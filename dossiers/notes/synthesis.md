# Local and global converse, Paley–Wiener levels, and spectral jets

Technical note, 21 November 2026. All page numbers refer to the 940-page Masullo attachment of 20 November. Distinguish the identity asserted *in a proof* from the theorem it is used to prove. The two can have different truth values.

## Established 1. A nonzero residual in the proof of the Global Converse Theorem

Theorem 6.3.2.1, pp. 304–307, equations (6.3.2.10–13). Set \(F=\mathbb Q\), \(S=\{\infty\}\), \(\chi=1\), so \(t_0=0\), \(\delta_\chi=1\), and the outside-\(S\) Euler factor is the ordinary \(\zeta(s)\). Choose a nonzero even smooth function \(h\ge0\) with support in \(1<|y|<2\). Set
\[
g(y)=h(y)-2h(2y),\qquad f=g\otimes1_{\widehat{\mathbb Z}}.
\]
Both \(f(0)=0\) and \(\widehat f(0)=\int f=0\); this is precisely the restriction (6.3.2.12). Put \(H(s)=\int_{\mathbb R^\times} h(y)|y|^s d^\times y\) with the manuscript's real Haar normalization. Unfolding and continuation give, up to the same positive Haar constant,
\[
 Z(f,s)=(1-2^{1-s})\zeta(s)H(s)=\eta(s)H(s).
\]
Here \(\eta\) is the Dirichlet eta function. The finite test is self-Fourier and the global functional equation gives \(Z(\widehat f,1-s)=Z(f,s)\). Therefore (6.3.2.10–11) produces on the common strip the pair \(((1-s)Z(f,s),sZ(f,s))\). The operator (6.3.2.13) subtracts one component from the other. At \(s=1/4\) it gives \(\frac12\eta(1/4)H(1/4)>0\). Indeed the alternating eta series is positive, already bounded below by \(1-2^{-1/4}>0\), and \(H(1/4)>0\). This refutes the explicit assertion that the image lands in the kernel. It does so even on the polar-free component; neither residues nor a choice of conjugation conventions is at issue.

The proof on p. 306 then identifies the common pair with the restriction of one entire function. This step fails. The range description of Theorem 6.3.2.1 is used in §7.1.4, pp. 326–330, and in the spectral construction on p. 441. This counterexample does **not** refute the corrected range description. Matching the two coordinates by \(sF=(1-s)G\) on the overlap, or cancelling the pole factors on the polar-free domain before gluing, removes the displayed mismatch. Any corrected proof must still justify continuity in strip seminorms, closed range and identification of all divisible functions; the scalar test neither proves nor disproves these additional steps. The two restrictions of any compatible pair in the original *unweighted* diagonal gluing do give a unique global strip-Schwartz function by holomorphic gluing and a finite cover of each closed vertical strip. Failure is in compatibility of the constructed pair.

**Independent literal error on p. 304.** For \(\chi|_{C_F^1}\ne1\), (6.3.2.6) is the zero map to \(\mathbb C^2\), so (6.3.2.5) cannot be exact at its right endpoint. An even nontrivial quadratic character over \(\mathbb Q\), e.g. the character modulo five, is enough. The immediately following (6.3.2.7) already drops the two-dimensional space, furnishing the natural correction. In the trivial isotype the residues map in (6.3.2.8) is written on a middle space intersected with the *entire* space \(AS(\mathbb C)\), so both residues would vanish there as well. The later prose describes meromorphic elements, compatible with the intended polar middle term. Neither display ambiguity repairs the nonzero residual test above.

## Established 2. The quotient in (7.3.3.5–6) fails the subspace test

Definition 7.3.3.3, p. 359, forming the spaces of Theorem 7.3.3.4 on pp. 360–361, divides the level at \(q^{-1}X\) by the image of the level at \(X\). Proposition 7.3.3.2, pp. 357–359, instead maps level \(X\) to level \(qX\). The changed direction has a detectable consequence. Fix \(F=\mathbb Q\), \(\chi=1\), \(S=\{\infty\}\), \(S_0=\{2\}\), \(S'=\{\infty,2\}\), \(X=Y=1\). Both sets are saturated where required, and \(\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb Z)=1\). Absolute and invariant seed constraints coincide when \(S\) has one place.

Let \(h\) be an even smooth function, supported in \((-1,1)\), equal to zero near zero, of integral zero and with \(h(7/8)=1\). For example on the positive half-line subtract two translates of the same smooth bump with height one and support radius \(1/32\), at \(7/8\) and \(5/8\), then reflect evenly. Put \(g=\mathcal F_\infty^{-1}h\). Thus \(g(0)=\widehat g(0)=0\). The definition (7.3.2.2) prints the finite seed \(r=1_{\mathbb Z_2^\times}\); the discussion of transition operators and the proof, p. 358, use \(r=1_{\mathbb Z_2}\). Both readings fail. Write \(f=g\otimes r\).

For \(t>0\) let \(L_t(f)=\Phi_{\mathbb Q,S'}(\widehat f)(t,1)\) in the trivial compact type. This is a continuous linear measurement on the coinvariant: Fourier transformation descends to it (the norm of an \(S'\)-unit is one), and evaluation of the theta transform at a fixed idele is continuous. Explicitly the orbit is \(\{\pm2^k:k\in\mathbb Z\}\). An angular average over \(J^1\) does not affect the unit-invariant even seed here; generally the same support bounds survive averaging, since representatives with real coordinate one and 2-adic unit component suffice. With the self-dual measure \(\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb Z_2)=1\) and the standard character,
\[
 U(t)=L_t(f)=
 \begin{cases}
 2\sum_{k\ge0} h(2^k t),&r=1_{\mathbb Z_2},\\
 \sum_{k\ge0} h(2^k t)-h(t/2),&r=1_{\mathbb Z_2^\times}.
 \end{cases}
\]
The second formula uses \(\widehat{1_{\mathbb Z_2^\times}}=1_{\mathbb Z_2}-\frac12 1_{2^{-1}\mathbb Z_2}\). For any seed at level \(X\), \(U(t)=0\) for \(t>X\) in the ball reading, and for \(t>2X\) in the unit reading. These vanishings persist on the closed level, by continuity. Non-even seeds produce the sum of the two real branches and satisfy the same bounds.

By the definition \(PV_2 f=f-T_2f\) on p. 358 with \(T_2f(x_2)=f(x_2/2)\), Fourier scaling and \(S'\)-unit invariance give
\[
 L_t(PV_2f)=U(t)-\tfrac12 U(t/2).
\]
For the ball put \(t=7/4\): \(U(t)=0\), \(U(t/2)=2\), and the result is \(-1\). For the units put \(t=7/2\): \(U(t)=0\), \(U(t/2)=-1\), and the result is \(1/2\). Both results violate even the upper bound of the original level \(1\), hence certainly of level \(1/2\). Rescaling the bump and the test point gives the same obstruction for every \(X>0\). Thus the printed denominator is not a subspace of the numerator. The resulting orbital basis statement has no defined quotient with these formulas. Section 8.1 (p. 381) and p. 393 discuss the resulting quotient. Reversing the scale to \(PW_X/PV_2PW_{X/2}\), or \(PW_{2X}/PV_2PW_X\), makes inclusion consistent with the proposition. Closed range, surjectivity onto any claimed quotient and Schauder basis remain separate requirements.

## Established under the literal insertion only. Density of both Paley–Wiener families

Propositions 7.3.2.2(1) and 7.3.2.4(1), pp. 352, 356. Under (7.3.2.2) and (7.3.2.13) as printed, every image uses \(1_{\mathbb Z_2^\times}\). Its local Tate factor is constant, so for \(s_0=2\pi i/\log2\) the residue there is zero, as the real-place Mellin transform of a Schwartz function is holomorphic at this nonzero imaginary point. The residue of the semi-local Mellin integral is a continuous functional on the fixed-character coinvariant: on finite test tensors this follows from the Laurent coefficient of the local geometric progression times the archimedean Mellin functional, hence on every compact-open Schwartz stage by continuity of the respective local functionals, and then by the final topology. It descends through the theta quotient since Mellin annihilates its kernel in the right half-plane and meromorphic continuation preserves that identity. Closure and union preserve vanishing.

To separate the full target \(S_0\) use \((q(y)-2q(2y))\otimes 1_{\mathbb Z_2}\) with \(q\) even nonnegative smooth supported close enough to \(\pm1\) that its Mellin integral \(H(s_0)\) has positive real part. The polar-free conditions hold as before. Now the semi-local integral is \((1-2^{1-s})H(s)/(1-2^{-s})\), whose residue at \(s_0\) is \(-H(s_0)/\log2\ne0\). This contradicts both printed density assertions. The unit-versus-ball discrepancy is material. If the finite insertion is the standard ball, this residue test no longer separates the spaces. In fact adjacent proofs assume precisely the ball insertion. Furthermore the Fréchet separation argument alone does not rule out density in all of the later weighted Hilbert completions. Page 488 cites the Fréchet density, and a repair must specify consistently which levels are meant.

## Established 3. Failure of invariance on the primal truncated jets

Theorem 7.4.1.1(2), pp. 366–367. Choose \(a=0,\,N=1\), trivial character over \(F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2,\zeta_3)\). If \(K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)\), then \(F/K\) is cyclic cubic. For a nontrivial cubic character \(\psi\) of this extension, induction or abelian factorization and conjugation of ideals give \(\zeta_F=\zeta_K L_K(\psi)^2\): the action of a transposition on the normal cyclic subgroup in \(S_3\) is inversion. The usual Hecke zero-counting formula supplies \(\rho\) in the critical strip with \(L_K(\rho,\psi)=0\), so \(\zeta_F\) has multiplicity at least two there. No critical-line or simplicity assumption enters.

Let \(V\) be the closed span of Masullo's advertised **primal** vectors \(\psi_{\lambda,0}\) for the untruncated biorthogonal system of Theorem 7.1.4.2(3), pp. 326–327. Grant all these vectors. The continuous derivative evaluation \(D'_\rho\) kills every displayed generator and therefore kills \(V\). Meanwhile \(D_\rho\psi_{\rho,0}=1\). In log-norm coordinates choose \(v\in C_c^\infty((1,2))\) of integral one, set \(b(x)=e^{-\rho x}v(x)\), and take the smooth radial convolution test \(\phi=-b'-\rho b\) divided by the compact-fibre volume. Its Mellin transform is \(\Phi(s)=(s-\rho)\int b(x)e^{sx}\,dx\). In particular \(\Phi(\rho)=0\) and \(\Phi'(\rho)=1\). Mellin convolution is multiplication, so
\[
 D'_\rho(A(\phi)\psi_{\rho,0})=\Phi'(\rho)D_\rho\psi_{\rho,0}+\Phi(\rho)D'_\rho\psi_{\rho,0}=1 .
\]
Consequently \(A(\phi)V\not\subset V\). Even granting all the preceding constructions, the endomorphism obtained by restriction in the theorem does not exist. The two-jet model is multiplication by \(t\) in \(\mathbb C[t]/t^2\) acting on the subspace spanned by \(1\). The span of the *dual* evaluations of order less than \(N\) is invariant, and low-order jets admit a quotient action. A compression is also a different construction. None gives the stated restriction. Page 366 explicitly says Theorem 7.4.1.1 will never be used; this counterexample is not an independent disproof of the main trace comparison. It is a separate arithmetic use of the known repeated-zero construction, not a new discovery of multiple Dedekind zeta zeros.

## Established 4. The stated boundary orbit has the wrong topology

Lemma 8.2.0.3(2), pp. 385–386, Definitions 8.2.0.1–2. Take \(F=\mathbb Q\) and the real place. Write \(e_\infty=(0,1_f)\). Its norm-one idelic orbit \(D_\infty^1\) consists of all \((0,u_f)\) with \(u_f\) a finite idele: the missing real coordinate can supply any positive norm. Let \(x_p=(0,u^{(p)})\) where \(u^{(p)}_p=p\), all other finite coordinates one. As \(p\) tends through the primes, \(x_p\to e_\infty\) in the additive adelic topology (outside any fixed finite set all coordinates are integral). The subset \(U=\{0\}\times\widehat{\mathbb Z}^\times\subset D^1\) is open in the claimed quotient topology. Under the map \(G^1\times Z_F\to D^1\) its preimage consists exactly of pairs \((g,e_\infty)\) with all finite components of \(g\) units, open in \(G^1\times Z_F\); the real coordinate isolates \(e_\infty\) in \(Z_F\). Yet \(U\) contains the limit and contains none of the \(x_p\). Hence the topologies differ.

Moreover \(D_\infty^1\) is closed in the subspace \(D^1\) because it is exactly its real-coordinate-zero locus. In any additive subspace neighborhood of \(e_\infty\) choose a basic neighborhood contained therein fixing finitely many finite coordinates and requiring only integrality elsewhere. Change successively the coordinates at the first \(n\) primes outside that set from one to the corresponding prime. These points of the neighborhood converge in the ambient adeles to an element with infinitely many finite nonunit coordinates. This limit is not in the orbit. No compact neighborhood in the orbit can contain the sequence, since its image would be compact and closed in the ambient Hausdorff adeles. Thus this closed subspace and hence \(D^1\) fail local compactness. The fact that norm-one *ideles themselves* carry the additive subspace topology is consistent with this example; passing to the orbit erases the compensating real coordinate. One may equip the orbit with its quotient topology, which changes the manuscript's topology. The interpretive orbital discussion of Chapter 8 is explicitly sidelined on p. 384. Also in Proposition 8.2.0.4, p. 386, uniqueness only modulo finite local units is false for the full ideles spelled out there: both \(g=1\) and the local uniformizer at \(v\) fix \(e_v\). Requiring the translating idele to have norm one removes this particular stabilizer objection.

Other checks: In Theorem 7.1.4.2(1), pp. 326–328, the quotient of entire functions by a discrete zero divisor has a product of finite jet spaces, whereas its continuous dual is a locally convex direct sum. ## Independent cross-check of two further Chapter 7 findings

**Small-norm asymptotics.** In Theorem 7.1.2.3, pp. 317–318, the first sum in (7.1.2.3) requires positive integers \(m<A\); it excludes zero. Take \(F=\mathbb Q\), \(S=\{\infty\}\), \(\chi=1\), \(A=1/2\) and \(f(y)=e^{-\pi y^2}\). This is an allowed Schwartz test; there is no polar-free hypothesis. The \(S\)-unit group is \(\{\pm1\}\), and the norm-one semilocal idele class group is trivial. The left side is \(2e^{-\pi g^2}\) on positive \(g\) (up to any common positive compact-average normalization), while the finite-place sum is empty and there is no positive integer less than \(1/2\). Thus the formula requires a function with nonzero limit to be \(O(g^{1/2})\).

Even after adding the constant and using the actual Laurent principal-part coefficients, allowing a polar line as remainder boundary is not valid in general. For \(F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\), with both real places in \(S\), no finite places and trivial compact character, take the product of real Gaussians. Unfolding the radial semilocal Mellin integral gives \(c\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s/2)^2\) with \(c>0\); there is a double pole at \(s=-2\). Subtract the entire polynomial in \(\log t\) accounting for the pole at zero on \(0<t<1\). Its Mellin transform has poles only at zero. The Mellin integral on \(t\ge1\) extends holomorphically near \(-2\): absolute convergence for \(\Re s>0\) follows from the positive Gaussian Tate integral, and on \(t\ge1\) continues to hold at every smaller real part with all logarithmic derivatives. If the remaining function on \((0,1)\) were \(O(t^2)\), its Mellin integral on the real axis as \(s\downarrow-2\) would be \(O((s+2)^{-1})\), impossible. The residue at \(-2\) produces a \(t^2\log t\) term. Thus the strict \(m<A\) sum cannot allow arbitrary \(A>0\) merely by restoring \(m=0\). Choose a nonpolar boundary or explicitly handle all boundary logarithms. Page 316 calls this result a motivation; Corollary 7.1.3.3 does invoke it on p. 325. These failures neither establish nor disprove the coarser corollary in every case.

**Polar multiplicity for odd infinity type.** Theorem 7.1.3.2(2), p. 324, allows every continuous unitary Hecke character unramified outside \(S\). For the primitive Dirichlet character modulo four over \(\mathbb Q\), choose \(S=\{\infty,2\}\). Its real component is the sign character. The real Tate factor has a simple pole at \(s=-1\), since its numerator is proportional up to a nowhere-zero holomorphic factor to \(\Gamma((s+1)/2)\) while the dual gamma factor at \(1-s\) is finite and nonzero there. The ramified factor at 2 is entire and nonzero. This is one genuine pole where the theorem requires order at most \(r_2=0\) because \(-1\) is negative odd. In fact p. 317 already lists the odd progression for the real sign character. Include odd-signature real places in the odd-pole count; the coarse upper bound \(r_1+r_2\le[F:\mathbb Q]\) off the imaginary axis still holds.
