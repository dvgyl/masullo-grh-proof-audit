# The polar-free domain and the second structure theorem

A technical supplement for the 940-page manuscript of Alessandro Maria Masullo. Citations are to the printed pages. The tests below use only elementary functions, the local Mellin integral and the gamma recurrence. Normalizing a Haar measure multiplies the indicated nonzero transforms by a fixed positive constant and leaves the conclusions unchanged.

## 1. A member of the prescribed finite-place factor has a natural boundary

**Statements.** Remark 2.1.2.1, p. 63, explicitly distinguishes the multiplicative Bruhat space from compactly supported smooth functions. It identifies the unit-invariant profiles in the multiplicative space with rapidly decreasing sequences on the valuation group. Definition 5.3.4.1, p. 259, (5.3.4.1), uses this multiplicative space as a subspace of the *additive* Schwartz–Bruhat space. Lemma 5.3.4.2(1–3), pp. 259–261, asserts closed inclusion, entireness on the space of semilocal quasicharacters and stability under multiplication by each quasicharacter trivial on the group of S-units.

**Admissible inputs for the displayed tensor expression.** Take \(F=\mathbb Q\), \(S=\{\infty,2\}\), let \(0\ne\eta\in C_c^\infty((1,2))\) be nonnegative, and take the simple tensor \(f(x,y)=\eta(x)h(y)\). For the normalized valuation \(v_2(2)=1\), set
\[
 h(y)=\begin{cases}2^{-2^j},&v_2(y)=4^j,\quad j=1,2,\ldots,\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\]
All real jets of \(\eta\) vanish at zero. The finite factor is invariant under \(\mathbb Z_2^\times\), and for each integer \(N\ge0\) the numbers \((1+4^j)^N 2^{-2^j}\) tend to zero. Thus this simple tensor belongs to the right-hand tensor product of (5.3.4.1) regardless of its completion.

1. Additive Schwartz–Bruhat functions on \(\mathbb Q_2\) are locally constant everywhere. The values at the two sequences \(y_j=2^{4^j}\), \(y'_j=2^{4^j+1}\) approaching zero are positive and zero, respectively. No extension can be locally constant at zero. For real \(x\) with \(\eta(x)>0\) this also disproves the mixed additive inclusion.
2. The quasicharacter \(c(x,y)=|x|^{-1}|y|_2^{-1}\) is trivial on \(\{\pm 2^k:k\in\mathbb Z\}\), exactly as required. At valuation \(4^j\) its finite factor sends the profile to \(2^{4^j-2^j}\). Even boundedness fails. Evaluation of the real variable at any point with positive bump value is a continuous tensor slice, so the multiplied function cannot belong to the product space.
3. With unit shell mass one, the directly convergent integral at the norm character \(|\cdot|^s\) on \(\Re s>0\) is
\[
 Z(s)=B(s)H(s),\qquad
 B(s)=\int_1^2\eta(x)x^s\frac{dx}{x},\qquad
 H(s)=\sum_{j\ge1} 2^{-2^j}2^{-4^j s}.
\]
Here \(B\) is entire and \(B(0)>0\). The imaginary axis is a natural boundary even for meromorphic continuation. For integers \(m\) and \(r\ge0\), take \(s_0=2\pi i m/(4^r\log2)\). For \(j\ge\max(1,r)\) every summand has phase one at \(s_0\). Remove the finitely many earlier terms, an entire function. The differentiated tail of each fixed order \(k\) converges uniformly on the closed right half-plane by the Weierstrass test. For \(k=2^j\) with \(j\ge\max(1,r)\), all terms of the tail derivative have the same sign at \(s_0\), and the \(j\)-th alone gives
\[
  |H_{\rm tail}^{(k)}(s_0)|\ \ge\ (\log 2)^k\,k^{2k}/2^k.
\]
An extension holomorphic in a disk of radius \(R>0\) gives a bound \(Mk!R^{-k}\) on a smaller circle, which is impossible because \(k!\le k^k\). A pole at \(s_0\) is impossible as well, by the bounded one-sided limit of the original series. The specified points are dense on the imaginary axis. Local meromorphic continuation of \(B H\) through any boundary segment would give meromorphic continuation of \(H\) there by division by the nonzero entire function \(B\).

An independent check uses the Hadamard gap theorem. For the power series in \(w=2^{-s}\) the nonzero degrees have ratio 4, and \( (2^{-2^j})^{1/4^j}=2^{-2^{-j}}\to1\), so its convergence circle has radius one and is a natural boundary. Absolute summability on that circle excludes boundary poles. The elementary derivative argument above supplies a proof for this particular series without invoking the gap theorem.

**Significance and repair.** All three assertions fail under the manuscript's explicit multiplicative convention. If the claimed additive inclusion takes precedence, this example instead demonstrates that the displayed tensor domain must be reduced. There is no counterexample here to Tate continuation for genuine additive tests. Replacing the finite factors with \(C_c^\infty(\mathbb Q_p^\times)\), extended by zero, gives finite sums over valuation shells. This eliminates this obstruction and supplies the finite support used in the proof on p. 261. The proof of (7.3.1.18), pp. 345–346, specifically relies on Lemma 5.3.4.2(3) and Lemma 5.3.4.3. The corrected source and the claimed image and topology must be related by an argument on the corrected domain.

**A separate one-way implication.** In proving Lemma 5.3.4.2(2), p. 260, the manuscript also infers that the original test is flat from entireness of its transform. For \(F=\mathbb Q\), \(S=\{\infty\}\), \(x e^{-\pi x^2}\) is odd and has derivative 1 at zero. All allowed semilocal quasicharacters are even, since \(-1\) is an S-unit. Thus its integral vanishes identically on the entire semilocal quasicharacter space. The claim there that restriction from \(\Omega(J_{F,S})\) to \(\Omega(\mathbb R^\times)\) is surjective also misses the sign component. The later Proposition 5.3.4.4(2), pp. 263–264, asks for *a representative* with the same Mellin transform. For this odd test the zero representative works. The example cannot refute that later equivalence.

## 2. The lexicographic filtration is incompatible with its pole sets

The proof of Theorem 5.4.1.1 specifies the lexicographic order on the multi-indices on p. 270. Its definition of \(U^{\le n}\), equation (5.4.1.14), instead uses separate coordinate cutoffs. Page 271 asserts that \(n'\le n\) implies \(U^{\le n'}\subset U^{\le n}\), and uses this for the filtration (5.4.1.15). Page 273 invokes the resulting cofiltered diagram.

Use \(F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\), its two real places, \(S=S_\infty\), and trivial isotype. Every local factor has poles at \(0,-2,-4,\ldots\). In the chosen enumeration, \((0,2)<_{\rm lex}(1,0)\). Yet
\[
 U^{\le(0,2)}=\{0,-2,-4\},\qquad U^{\le(1,0)}=\{0,-2\}.
\]
This affects actual classes. Put \(G(x_1,x_2)=\exp[-\pi(x_1^2+x_2^2)]\) and \(q=x_1^4x_2^4G\). Its Mellin transform is a nonzero constant times \(\pi^{-s-4}\Gamma((s+4)/2)^2\). It is holomorphic at zero and \(-2\), with a double pole at \(-4\). Thus the class belongs to the filtration at \((1,0)\) and fails the condition at \((0,2)\). In particular the natural quotient map associated with the claimed containment need not exist. With componentwise order the multi-indices form a directed set and their pole sets are nested in the required sense. That repair leaves the minimal-index difficulty below.

## 3. A single standard Gaussian cannot span two independent polar coefficients

In the same real quadratic setting, the minimal cutoff \(n=(0,0)\) tests precisely the principal part at zero. By (5.4.1.15–20), pp. 271–272, all classes holomorphic there disappear from the relevant finite quotient. With no finite places, the finite-prime principal-value operator is the identity. Equations (5.4.1.22–23), pp. 273–274, assert generation of the target quotient by the images of seeds with \(0\le n'\le n\). At the least index only the Gaussian \(G\) is available, up to its fixed normalization.

Nevertheless both \(G\) and \(x_1^2G\) are genuine additive Schwartz functions. Their transforms are
\[
 Z_G(s)=C\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s/2)^2,\qquad
 Z_{x_1^2G}(s)=\frac{s}{2\pi}Z_G(s),\quad C>0 .
\]
These follow by direct integration over \(\mathbb R^\times\) in both places and the gamma recurrence. Passing to the trivial character and the theta image leaves these Mellin values unchanged by unfolding. Each genuine pair satisfies the semilocal functional equation on the overlap. The first transform has a double pole at zero, the second a simple pole with nonzero residue. Their principal parts are linearly independent, even after arbitrary addition of entire functions. The quotient therefore has at least two independent classes and one generator cannot span it. In any totally real degree \(d\), the tests \(G\prod_{i=1}^j x_i^2\) for \(0\le j<d\) give the distinct orders \(d,d-1,\ldots,1\).

**Finite repair.** If a seed \(Z_0\) has the maximum allowed pole at each of finitely many distinct nodes \(s_i\), its semilocal norm dilates generate functions \(e^{jts}Z_0(s)\) for \(j\ge0\) after harmless normalization of the constant character phase. Pick \(t>0\) for which all \(e^{ts_i}\) are distinct. The map \(s\mapsto e^{ts}\) is a local coordinate at each node. Hermite interpolation gives a polynomial \(P\) with arbitrary specified Taylor jets in that coordinate. Then \(P(e^{ts})Z_0(s)\) realizes prescribed principal parts at the finitely many nodes. In particular \((e^{ts}-1)Z_G\) supplies the missing class at zero. This proves finite interpolation under the stated seed hypothesis. It gives no uniform estimate as the nodes increase.

**The unresolved infinite step.** The proof on p. 273 asserts a surjection from the completion of \(H\) in its coordinate quotient topology onto \(H\) with its initial, generally finer, quotient locally convex topology. No continuity of the identity in that direction or compatible lifting estimate is supplied there. For example, \(\mathbb C^{(\mathbb N)}\) with its strict LF topology is complete, projects onto each finite coordinate space and embeds densely in \(\mathbb C^{\mathbb N}\) with the product topology. Its coordinate completion is the entire product. There is no continuous extension of the identity from that product to the original strict LF space, since composition with its inclusion into the product would be the identity on a dense set and hence everywhere. This invalidates the general inference. The situation of the particular theta space requires a separate infinite interpolation, splitting, or completeness argument. This model alone settles no surjectivity assertion for the actual theta space.

**An infinite repair in the actual quadratic example.** In the two-real-place trivial-character case above, arbitrary principal parts at all the points \(-2n\) can in fact be realized by a single genuine test. Let \(u\) be a smooth, even, compactly supported real function equal to one near zero. Take \(q\ge0\), smooth and compactly supported away from zero, with \(Q(s)=\int q(y)|y|^s\,d^\times y\) nonzero at all real \(s\). Use \(d^\times y=dy/|y|\); other constants are absorbed in coefficients. Write \(R(z)=\int u(x)|x|^z d^\times x=2/z+r_0+O(z)\) near zero. Splitting the integral where \(u=1\) proves that zero is its only pole. For \(A_{n,\epsilon}(x)=x^{2n}u(x/\epsilon)\), the Mellin transform equals \(\epsilon^z R(z)\) with \(z=s+2n\). Consequently the principal parts at \(-2n\) of \(A\otimes A\) and \(A\otimes q\), respectively, are
\[
 \frac4{z^2}+\frac{4r_0+8\log\epsilon}{z},\qquad
 \frac{2Q(-2n)}{z}.
\]
There are no poles elsewhere. To prescribe coefficients \(a_n/z^2+b_n/z\), take the combination with first coefficient \(a_n/4\) and second coefficient \([b_n-a_n(r_0+2\log\epsilon)]/[2Q(-2n)]\). For each fixed \(n\) these coefficients grow at most linearly in \(|\log\epsilon|\) as \(\epsilon\downarrow0\). For \(m\le n\), derivatives of \(A\) up to order \(m\) are \(O_n(\epsilon^{2n-m})\) by rescaling. For \(n\ge1\), choose \(0<\epsilon_n<1\) so the \(C^n\) norm of the resulting combination is at most \(2^{-n}\); handle \(n=0\) separately. The series of tests converges in every smooth seminorm on a common compact support. Laurent coefficients of the two-variable Mellin transform at each fixed point are continuous on this test space. Indeed Taylor subtraction to order exceeding \(2n\) in each coordinate, on a fixed product neighborhood of the axes with smooth cutoffs, expresses them as finite sums of evaluations, derivatives, and convergent integrals with locally integrable powers. Thus the sum has precisely the prescribed coefficients.

There is also a direct realization of the entire remainder on this same component. For a pair in the graph (5.4.1.3), subtract the pair of the just-constructed test. The first component extends to an entire function \(E\). It is Schwartz on each bounded closed vertical strip, by the two half-plane hypotheses and the ordinary vertical Stirling estimates for \(\rho\) at sufficiently large imaginary part. Its inverse Mellin transform \(h\) and all logarithmic derivatives decay faster than every power of the norm at zero and infinity, by shifting to any fixed real line and differentiating the absolutely convergent inversion integral. For the nonnegative even cutoff \(q\) above, the test \(C h(|xy|)q(y)\), extended by zero on \(xy=0\), is smooth and Schwartz, with every jet on each axis zero. Substitution \(t=|xy|\) gives its transform \(E(s)\) after choosing the constant \(C\) to absorb \(2\int q(y)d^\times y\). The functional equation determines the second component on the overlap and hence everywhere in its half-plane. This proves the claimed *set-theoretic* range in this specific quadratic example by a different argument. The general field, all characters, finite-place factors, stated topological identifications and printed action are separate questions.

Page 271 gives \(\lim_{s\to s_0}(s-s_0)^m\phi(s)\) as the \(-m\) Laurent coefficient, even for \(m\) below the pole order. The displayed limit fails already for \(m=1\) and the Gaussian above. The subsequent contour extraction on the same page gives the correct functional.

## 4. Two incompatible target operations

**Half-plane reflection.** Theorem 5.4.2.2(3), p. 276, (5.4.2.6), with \(I=(0,\infty)\), calls \(J\phi(s)=\rho(s)\phi(1-s)\) a holomorphic isomorphism from \(AS(\Re s>0)\) to \(AS(\Re s<1)\). Put \(F=\mathbb Q\), \(S=S_\infty\), \(\chi=1\), and \(g(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\). The Gaussian source has that Mellin transform up to positive measure normalization. Stirling estimates on compact vertical strips put \(g\) in the specified input space. Under self-dual normalization \(\rho(s)=g(s)/g(1-s)\), whence \(Jg(s)=g(s)\) on the overlap. Its continued value has a pole at zero with residue 2. Any holomorphic continuation to the asserted target would agree there by uniqueness, an impossibility. A different real additive character rescales the gamma factor by a zero-free exponential factor and preserves the obstruction. Proposition 5.4.2.1, p. 275, restricts \(I\) to the common strip. Restriction of the output to \(0<\Re s<1\) also suffices to *write* the bottom difference arrow in (5.4.2.6). Thus this example refutes the stronger half-plane mapping and leaves the weaker overlap gluing to be justified.

**Action on the graph.** Theorem 5.4.1.1(1), p. 266, and Proposition 5.4.1.4, p. 267, define the two-component radial action on \(W\) as multiplication by \((t^s,t^{1-s})\), although (5.4.1.3) defines \(W\) by \(\phi(s)=\rho(s)\psi(s)\) on an open strip. At \(t=2,s=1/4\), take the same Gaussian and its Fourier mate. The old pair obeys this equality with both sides nonzero. The two new sides differ by \(2^{1/4}/2^{3/4}\). For the polar-free proposition use an even nonnegative bump supported on \(1<|x|<2\), whose Mellin transform is positive at \(1/4\). The actual dilation \(f(x)\mapsto f(x/t)\) has Mellin factor \(t^s\) for the first coordinate. Fourier scaling gives \(\widehat{f(\cdot/t)}(y)=t\widehat f(ty)\), so the second coordinate evaluated at \(1-s\) also acquires \(t^s\). This corrects this radial equivariance error without deciding the theorem's range and topology.
