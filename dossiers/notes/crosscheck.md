## Recheck of the arithmetic projection obstruction

Normalize Haar measure on the norm-one factor of the rational idèle classes to volume one. In the radial component at center \(a=1/2\) and Sobolev degree four, write \(w=e^{2\alpha |x|}(1+x^2)^2\), \(H=L^2(w\,dx)\), \(M\) for the closed twisted theta space, and \(P\) for the orthogonal projection onto \(M^\perp\). At a known critical zero \(1/2+i\gamma\), \(u=e^{-i\gamma x}/w\) belongs to \(M^\perp\). Indeed, the continuous pairing is radial Tate evaluation on the theta subspace and vanishes by divisibility by the Riemann zeta function. Equivalently one first unfolds radialized finite tests that are linear combinations of indicators of \(d\widehat{\mathbb Z}\) on the half-plane of absolute convergence, and then continues to the zero.

Take \(b\ge0\) smooth, of integral one, compactly supported in \((1,2)\) in the log variable. For \(\phi(x)=e^{-i\gamma x}b(x)\), ordinary left convolution gives \(wAu=e^{-i\gamma x}D(x)\), where \(D(x)=\int b(t)w(x)/w(x-t)\,dt\). There is a nonzero \(h\) in \(M\) whose unweighted Fourier transform is entire. In fact, take even \(q\ge0\) smooth, nonzero, supported in \(1<|t|<3/2\), and \(f=q-2q(2\cdot)\). Its value and integral vanish at the two polar functionals. Then \(h(x)=e^{x/2}\sum_{n\ne0}f(ne^x)\) is nonzero at some \(1<e^x<3/2\), vanishes for large positive \(x\), and decays faster than every exponential at the other end by Poisson summation. Its translates are dense in unweighted \(L^2\) by Plancherel and isolated zeros of its nonzero entire transform.

If \(Au\in M^\perp\), translation invariance of \(M\) makes \(e^{-i\gamma x}(D(x+1)-D(x))\) orthogonal to all those translates. This difference belongs to unweighted \(L^2\) because \(D(x)=c_\pm+O(1/|x|)\) at the two ends, with \(c_\pm=\int b(t)e^{\pm 2\alpha t}dt\). Thus it vanishes. A continuous periodic function with a finite limit is constant, contradicting the distinct two limits when \(\alpha>0\) and \(D(0)<1=\lim_{+\infty}D\) at width zero. In particular \((1-P)APu\ne0\) under the manuscript’s actual arithmetic hypotheses. The smooth test works at every width.

Every lifted finite-rank operator in (10.4.3.43) takes values in \(M^\perp\). Their asserted projective norm limit (10.4.3.45) cannot be \(AP\). At width zero every Paley–Wiener projector in pp. 488–489 kills \(u\), while the claimed limiting operator sends \(u\) to \(M_\rho(1-P)Au\ne0\) for a sufficiently large fixed smooth admissible cutoff. This refutes the two displayed operator limits even pointwise. The argument does not ascertain inequality of their scalar traces.

## Chapter 10 power traces and a single-test inequality on the line

Let \(F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]{2},\zeta_3)\). Two conjugate nontrivial characters of its cubic extension of \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)\) give identical Hecke L-functions by the Galois action on ideals. Consequently \(\zeta_F=\zeta_{\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)} L(\psi)^2\). Pick a nontrivial zero \(\beta+i\gamma\) of the nontrivial Hecke factor. Set \(a=\beta\), Sobolev degree \(s=3\), and consider the trivial compact character. A frequency point supports only an ordinary evaluation in the Hilbert dual: the first polynomial jet has infinite squared norm \(\int x^2(1+x^2)^{-3/2}dx\); higher ones also fail. Yet p. 448 counts two copies at this zero.

Here a fixed autocorrelation test disproves the scalar trace formula itself. Use a logarithmic bump whose multiplier is nonzero at \(\gamma\) and take its convolution with its involution. Its multiplier \(B\) is nonnegative, rapidly decreasing, real analytic, and positive at \(\gamma\). Normalize the compact average by the reciprocal of its Haar volume. The dual quotient embeds as a closed subspace \(E\) of radial distributions in physical \(L^2((1+x^2)^{-3/2}dx)\). A Gaussian Tate seed at infinity times the finite unit-ball indicators has transform equal to \(\Lambda_F(z)\) times a nonvanishing exponential and constant. Apply \((1-D_c)(1-cD_c)\), where \(D_cf(x)=f(cx)\) with the idelic multiplier having norm \(c>1\). This kills the value at zero and the integral. The angular average of its theta function belongs to the geometric subspace and its transform on \(\Re z=a\) has exactly the Dedekind zero ordinates, since \((1-c^{-z})(1-c^{1-z})\) is nonzero there. Translating and pairing against \(E\) shows that Fourier transforms of elements of \(E\) have support in this discrete set \(Z\). This inclusion alone is enough.

Choose a smooth, locally finite frequency partition \(\sum_j\chi(\xi-j)=1\), with fixed compact support and uniformly bounded derivatives. Each localized multiplier \(B_j=\chi(\cdot-j)B\) has norm \(O_N((1+|j|)^{-N})\) on the ambient weighted dual. Its convolution kernel has this bound in the \((1+|x|)^{3/2}\)-weighted \(L^1\) norm by four integrations by parts, and translations have the corresponding polynomial bound. Its range on \(E\) has dimension at most \(\#(Z\cap\operatorname{supp}\chi_j)\). This follows by localizing individual points, using the structure theorem for point-supported distributions and the divergent jet norm. The crude global zero count bounds these ranks by \(O((2+|j|)\log(2+|j|))\). Compose with the ambient orthogonal projection to \(E\) if needed. The localized operators then sum in trace norm to the actual multiplier on \(E\), so the original quotient action is trace class.

For any nonzero eigenvalue, the dual generalized eigenvectors have Fourier support at the finitely many nodes \(t\) with \(B(t)=\lambda\). Each node contributes at most one dimension; every nonzero eigenvalue is positive real. By Lidskii’s theorem the actual trace is at most \(\sum_{t\in Z}B(t)\), indexed by distinct points. The printed right hand side is strictly larger because every point is counted at least once and \(\gamma\) is counted twice with \(B(\gamma)>0\). Thus (10.3.1.3) fails on one admissible smooth compact test for which nuclearity is independently justified. This counterexample at \(s=3\) does not refute the analogous trace at \(s=4\).

At Sobolev degree \(s=3\), for any strictly positive strip width use the upper boundary \(a=\beta-\alpha\), or the lower boundary and reflected tests to keep \(a>0\). At the upper edge take the compact radial test \(\phi(x)=e^{-(\alpha+i\gamma)x}k(x)/V\) with \(k\ge0\) smooth in \((1,2)\), \(\int k=1\); \(V\) is the angular Haar volume. At every other point of the strip its Mellin multiplier has modulus strictly less than one, by positivity of the log support and strict triangle inequality. If nuclearity and the trace formula on p. 454 both hold, application to convolution powers and dominated convergence gives \(\lim_n\operatorname{Tr}(T^n)=2\).

Independently, a generalized eigenvector with \(|\lambda|\ge1\) has only the trivial angular component. Divide it by \(e^{(\alpha+i\gamma)x}\). Its equation becomes \((P-\lambda)^m u=0\) with \(Pu(x)=\int k(t)u(x+t)dt\). Every \(u_j=(P-\lambda)^j u\) is polynomially weighted square integrable on the positive half-line. For negative \(x\), set \(n=\lceil-x\rceil+1\). The finite negative binomial identity expresses \(u\) as a sum of \(\lambda^{-n-j}{-n\choose j}P^n u_j\). The support of \(k^{*n}\) puts these averages in \([0,|x|+4]\); its supremum is bounded by \(\|k\|_\infty\). Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the averages by \(O((1+|x|)^2)\) and the binomials grow polynomially. This proves temperedness on the previously uncontrolled negative end. Fourier support now gives no eigenvalue of modulus at least one except one. At one the only admissible polynomial is constant by the positive-tail jet bound. Its algebraic multiplicity is at most one. Under the trace-class assertion, Lidskii’s theorem and summability of eigenvalue moduli yield \(\lim_n\operatorname{Tr}(T^n)\le1\), contradicting the printed value. This is a contradiction to the conjunction of trace class and the printed scalar formula at positive width. It does not independently prove nuclearity at positive width or put a double zero on an edge of a strip with preassigned center one half.

The corrected strict edge count and the prospect of a resolvent proof were public before this cross-check. They should be credited separately from the direct fixed-test trace inequality and the positive-width test by powers. I found no exact independent antecedent of these particular arguments among the files inspected. Independent mathematical arguments above are not machine-checked.

## Independent check of the public CM witness for Chapter 13

For \(n=1254\), the two points \((-912,25992)\) and \((-528,26136)\) on \(y^2=x(x-n)(x+n)\) satisfy the equation exactly, with both sides respectively 675584064 and 683090496. The halving-descent homomorphism to square classes sends them to \(-57,-33\). The full torsion image is contained in the subgroup generated by \(-1,n\): the three nontrivial two-torsion classes \(-1,n,-n\) are nonsquares, so none is a double and no order-four point exists; odd-order torsion maps trivially. The two classes and their product \(11\cdot19\) are independent modulo that subgroup, giving rank at least two. For the squarefree congruent-number twist \(1254\equiv6\pmod8\) the sign is negative. Rank one would follow from analytic rank one by the Gross–Zagier–Kolyvagin theorem. Hence the analytic order is at least three. The CM correspondence turns this into a unitary Hecke character over \(\mathbb Q(i)\) with a zero of that order at \(s=1/2\). No use of BSD or GRH is involved. [Primary public derivation, folio 522g](https://github.com/Titulus233/The-Music-of-the-Primes-Revise/blob/b8900893b1083ed2d4110b51b1e722ab15cb0f91/src/the-music-of-the-primes-revised.tex) · [Gross’s Theorem 3.3](https://abel.math.harvard.edu/~gross/preprints/lectures-pcmi.pdf) · [Congruent-number CM character](https://intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/iccm/2017/0005/0001/ICCM-2017-0005-0001-a003.pdf)

A normalization detail matters for the *printed* p. 522. The independent revision makes a canonical global normalization in stating its final residue. With Masullo’s choices in (A.3.2.1–2), p. 560, the norm-one volume for \(\mathbb Q(i)\) is \(V=\pi/4\), and the finite unit volumes equal one except at the unique place over 2. Use the permitted spiral section \(\tau(t)=(t^{1/2}e^{i\log t},1_f)\), let \(e=\pm1\) denote the angular weight and \(\kappa\) the restriction of the chosen Hecke character to the norm-one subgroup. A test with angular factor \(\bar\kappa\) and radial factor \(h=\partial_x^2(e^{-iex}b(x))\) for a unit-mass bump in \((1,2)\) kills both required moments in every compact isotype; so do its positive translates. The two pole integrals vanish by compact character orthogonality. The central zero is tested at \(z=ie\) with \(H(ie)=-1\). The coefficient in the normalized all-zero side minus the printed capped side is \(m-2V=m-\pi/2\ne0\). Corrections at the complex place and the single exceptional finite place are \(O(e^{-u/2})\) for a log translation by \(u\). At the finite place only a bounded number of shells with \(n\log 2=u+O(1)\) enter, each of size \(O(2^{-n/2})\). At the complex place the kernel is \(O(e^{-u/2})\) on the translated annulus of bounded multiplicative mass. Thus their translation Laplace transforms have no pole at \(ie\), while the isolated spectral coefficient does. This confirms the CM contradiction with Masullo’s own normalization as well as the canonical version.

The restriction to the critical line also has a classical independent check. Connes, *Trace formula in noncommutative geometry and the zeros of the Riemann zeta function*, equations (6)–(7), p. 10 of the arXiv preprint, uses the same polar-free seeds and the squared-norm weight \(|g|(1+\log^2|g|)^{\delta/2}\). Theorem 1, p. 13, counts a critical-line zero with the largest integer \(n<(1+\delta)/2\) not exceeding its analytic multiplicity; Corollary 2, p. 14, gives a trace-class operator with these multiplicities for compact smooth tests. Masullo's space \(H^3_{F,1/2}\) is exactly Connes's cokernel at \(\delta=3\): with \(a=1/2\) the weight in (9.2.2.41) equals Connes's weight, and both spaces are quotients by the closure of the polar-free theta functions. At \(\delta=3\) this means one copy, including at the CM central zero. Consequently its nontrivial angular component with a nonnegative radial Fourier multiplier that is positive at the central point gives an independent disproof of (10.3.1.3) at the preassigned center one half. Normalizing the compact angular test by its Haar volume avoids any convention change in the test action. [Connes, original article preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9811068#page=13)

## Chapter 4: the printed double logarithm and the super-Liouville example

Theorem 4.6.1.5, p. 212, defines \(b_k=\log|\gamma_k|_G\) and assumes \(\log b_k\in\mathbb Q\log b\). The sentence preceding the theorem says the same thing. The intrinsic frequency nodes of (4.6.1.12) are the annihilators of the cyclic subgroups and therefore have denominators \(b_k\), not \(\log b_k\). The separate printed assertion about the unique integer after (4.6.1.13) uses the wrong denominator. The later finiteness assertion (4.6.1.25), p. 215, requires commensurability of the \(b_k\) themselves. Correcting the auxiliary frequency notation does not cure the printed theorem.

Take \(G=\mathbb R_{>0}\) with identity norm, Haar measure \(dt/t\), trivial angular group and character. Set \(q_1=10\), \(q_{j+1}=10^{q_j^4}\), \(B=1+\sum_{j\ge1}1/q_j\), \(\gamma_1=e^B\), \(\gamma_2=e^{B^2}\). All assumptions in Situation 4.6.1.1 hold with the conventional zero phase; both periods exceed one, and their logarithms are \(\log B,2\log B\). Let \(p_j=q_j(1+\sum_{k\le j}1/q_k)\). The divisibilities among the \(q_j\) make \(p_j\) integral. The tail lies strictly between zero and \(2/q_{j+1}\). In particular \(B\) is irrational: a fixed rational of denominator \(d\) at a positive distance from \(p_j/q_j\) is at least \(1/(dq_j)\) away. The nodes \(y_j=2\pi q_j/B\), \(v_j=2\pi p_j/B^2\) are positive, distinct, and each has multiplicity one because the two lattices meet only at zero. Their distances satisfy \(0<\delta_j<4\pi q_j/(B^2 q_{j+1})\).

For a Mellin transform \(F\) of a log-coordinate test \(f\), define \(Q([F])=\sup_j|F(iy_j)-F(iv_j)|/\delta_j\). The numerator annihilates the product range because its multiplier is \((1-e^{-Bs})(1-e^{-B^2s})\). The seminorm before passage to the quotient is bounded by \(\int|x f(x)|dx\), which is continuous on the superexponentially decreasing test space. Thus \(Q\) is a continuous quotient seminorm even if one replaces the range by its closure. Any vector biorthogonal to the \(y_j\)-evaluation must have \(Q(\psi_{y_j,0})\ge1/\delta_j\). This violates the polynomial growth assertion in part (1). The actual test with transform \(F(s)=e^{s^2}\) is a log Gaussian. Its purported individual primal basis terms have seminorm at least \(\exp[-(2\pi q_j/B)^2]/\delta_j\to\infty\). Every convergent series has terms tending to zero. This disproves the asserted Schauder expansion under every enumeration and, a fortiori, the absolute basis assertion of (4.6.1.16). The rapid decrease of the coefficient samples along each separate lattice, part (2) with the natural indexing convention, survives this example. Arithmetic applications whose true log-periods are commensurate, such as residue norms which are powers of a single rational prime, require independent assessment.

The strong-dual assertion can also be checked directly. The continuous functional \(L=\sum_j2^{-j}(D_{y_j,0}-D_{v_j,0})/\delta_j\) converges in the strong dual by the bound above on every bounded set. Pairing with individual biorthogonal vectors forces coefficients \(2^{-j}/\delta_j\) at \(y_j\). The corresponding purported dual basis terms, evaluated on the one Gaussian, tend to infinity, hence do not converge to zero even weakly.
