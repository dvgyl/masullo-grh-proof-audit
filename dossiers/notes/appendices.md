# Contour shifts, boundary Euler products, and auxiliary trace facts

Technical note on the 20 November 2026 manuscript. External links below identify prior public objections. This note also checked Chapters 3, 7, 12 and 14 where they use the appendix machinery. It is a dependency audit, not a line-by-line certification of 940 pages.

## Established errors

### An asymptotic residue sum can diverge with only simple poles and uniformly bounded horizontal edges

Theorem B.3.2.1(1–2), pp. 605–606, equations (B.3.2.1–3), uses Definition B.3.1.1, p. 604. One must first correct a degeneracy in the definition: the printed estimate at k=0 forces the meromorphic function to vanish on a horizontal segment, and hence identically. The literal theorem under that hypothesis is vacuous. Below we use the intended bound at nonzero k (equivalently use 1+|k|). The conclusion allows *any* two interior vertical lines without poles. The definition only supplies *some* two vertical lines with controlled growth. The distinction persists when the horizontal heights are symmetric and linear, all poles are simple, and both the meromorphic function and its analytic weight are explicitly given.

Use the strip −2<Re z<3. For n≥2 put e_n=2^(−4n²), a_n=2^(2n²), and define

    Φ(z)=2^(z²),
    f(z)=Σ_{n≥2} a_n[(z−in−e_n)^(−1)−(z−in+e_n)^(−1)].

The paired n-th term is 2a_n e_n/[(z−in−e_n)(z−in+e_n)]. Its numerators are summable and both denominators grow on each fixed compact set for all sufficiently large n. This proves locally normal convergence off the indicated simple poles; the residues are ±a_n. On the horizontal lines of heights ±(k+1/2), k≥1, both denominator factors have magnitude at least 1/2, so the sum is bounded uniformly in k and Re z. The function is bounded on a neighborhood of each line as well. On the good vertical lines Re z=−3/2 and Re z=2 both factors are bounded away from zero uniformly. These lines satisfy Definition B.3.1.1(3) with ε₁=1/2, ε₂=1. The entire function Φ is uniformly Schwartz on every bounded strip.

For the theorem itself choose the pole-free lines Re z=0 and Re z=3/4 (ε₁=2, ε₂=9/4). Only ρ_n=in+e_n of each pair belongs to this open rectangle for heights above n. Its contribution to the claimed limit is

    a_n Φ(ρ_n)=2^(n²+e_n²) exp(2 i n e_n log 2).

The phase tends to 0. The real part therefore exceeds 2^(n²−1) for all sufficiently large n. Partial sums tend to infinity in real part. This disproves the intended part (1), even along the symmetric linear sequence of good heights.

On Re z=0 every paired summand of f equals −2a_n e_n/[(t−n)²+e_n²], a real negative value. For |t−n|≤e_n its absolute value is at least a_n/e_n, and Φ(it)=2^(−t²)≥2^(−n²−1). The integral of |Φf| there is at least a_n 2^(−n²), and summing these disjoint intervals gives infinity. This disproves the absolute convergence asserted in part (2). On the right edge the integral is absolutely convergent.

**Prior comparison and repair.** The public Lean issue already isolates missing side estimates, the k=0 defect, the use of upper instead of lower height-growth control, and the omitted higher-pole terms. The above construction is an explicit illustration that the residue *sum itself* diverges after the horizontal issues and the multiple-pole issue are removed; no priority claim. A corrected theorem selects sides on which Φf is integrable, controls the horizontal integrals, and sums Res(Φf). The ordinary residue theorem and dominated passage on the two sides then prove the assertion. Chapter 7 explicitly invokes the general theorem on p. 322. This example is a meromorphic function rather than a Hecke product, so each specific application requires its own contour estimates. It does not refute any arithmetic explicit formula that has these estimates. [Lean issue #1984](https://github.com/mccorvie/riemann-masullo/issues/1984) and ERRATA F17.

### The reciprocal Euler products converge uniformly across the pole ordinate

Remark B.7.2.7, p. 653, states that for a pure norm character the reciprocals of the finite Euler products converge pointwise on the 1-line, with failure of uniform convergence on any interval containing the pole ordinate. Over Q take χ=1, t₀=0. Write P_X(t)=∏_{p<X}(1−p^(−1−it)). For any bounded interval containing zero the convergence is in fact uniform, as follows.

Take δ=1/log X for X≥3. Euler expansion of log moduli at the absolutely convergent abscissa 1+δ gives

    |log |P_X(t)| − log |1/ζ(1+δ+it)|| ≤ C    (all real t, all X≥3).

Indeed, the total contribution of powers p^m, m≥2, is bounded by twice Σ_p Σ_{m≥2}p^(−m)/m. For p<X the m=1 discrepancy is at most δΣ_{p<X}(log p)/p=O(1), by Chebyshev's estimate for θ(x) and partial summation. For the missing primes p≥X it is bounded by Σ_{p≥X}p^(−1−δ)=O(1). To see the last bound, π(y)≪y/log y and partial summation give O(1)+(1+δ)O(∫_X^∞ y^(−1−δ)dy/log y), and u=δ log y turns that integral into ∫₁^∞e^(−u)du/u.

The reciprocal of ζ is holomorphic around 1 with a simple zero there, so |P_X(t)|≤C'(δ+|t|) whenever δ and |t| are small. The same estimate with δ=0 holds for |1/ζ(1+it)|. On |t|≤η this bounds the difference by C''(δ+η). On η≤|t|≤T the ordinary prime-norm-ordered Euler product converges uniformly, by Corollary B.7.2.5, p. 653, or by the prime number theorem with the classical error term and summation by parts. For the latter route, insert dθ(u)=du+d(θ(u)−u) in Σ(log p)/(p^(1+it)log p). The error tails are absolutely controlled after integration by parts by O_T(∫_X^∞exp(−c√log u)du/(u log u)), while ∫_X^∞u^(−1−it)du/log u=O(1/(η log X)) uniformly. Abel passage from Re s>1 identifies the limit. First take η small, then X large. This establishes the asserted uniform convergence across 0. Norm-character twisting shifts the ordinate. The special case suffices to refute the remark; the corrected conclusion strengthens the convergence available for reciprocals. Uniform convergence of P_X on compact sets does not imply uniform convergence of 1/P_X across its pole.

### Ordered Euler products do not define a Lebesgue integral at the 1-line

Proposition 3.4.2.1 and its proof, p. 145, invoke the prime-norm cutoff of (B.7.2.24), p. 653, to claim convergence of a net over *arbitrary finite subsets*. The divergence of the proposed Lebesgue integral in (3.4.2.1) was already recorded in Lean ERRATA I96. Take Q, f∞(u)=exp(−πu²), finite unit balls 1_{Z_p}, and c=|·|^(1+i); choose A=1/2. The modulus of the integrand is precisely that for the real exponent 1. The absolute integral diverges by Tonelli, since the archimedean factor is finite positive and finite-prime products ∏(1−1/p)^(−1) grow without bound. Thus a value obtained by truncating primes does not define that Lebesgue integral.

For an explicit unordered-product obstruction, take χ the even quadratic character modulo 5 and s=1, which selects a nontrivial component allowed by the theorem. Apart from p=5, the factors are (1−χ(p)/p)^(−1). For the subfamily χ(p)=1 the sum of their positive logs diverges by the prime number theorem in progressions (already the class 1 mod 5 suffices). For χ(p)=−1 the sum of negative logs diverges to −∞ (already the class 2 mod 5 suffices). Beyond any prescribed finite prime set one can multiply the partial product by >2 or by <1/2, hence the directed net cannot converge to the nonzero ordered product. An appropriate angular local test for the second illustration is 1_{Z_5^×} times the inverse unit character at 5. The first example already satisfies the Schwartz test conditions without a character twist. The analytic continuation on p. 144 or a specified prime-norm summation can still exist. [Public review of §3.4](https://github.com/mccorvie/riemann-masullo/blob/main/ERRATA.md).

### A norm twist invalidates the real-zero clause

Proposition B.6.1.1, p. 636, states in addition to a zero-free region that if L_f(σ,χ)=0 for σ in [1/2,1], then χ is quadratic and σ is unique and simple. Take F=Q and a positive ordinate γ with ζ(1/2+iγ)=0 (Hardy's theorem, independent of GRH). For the admissible unitary character χ=|·|^(iγ) one has L_f(s,χ)=ζ(s+iγ). Thus L_f(1/2,χ)=0. The square of χ at norm t equals t^(2iγ), a nonconstant character. The claimed quadratic conclusion fails. No multiplicity assumption is needed. This example attacks the real-zero clause on the entire half-strip. The possible exceptional real zero of a classical zero-free-region theorem is confined to that narrow region, with an appropriate normalized character and any norm twist tracked separately. B.6.1.5 invokes estimates at large heights, for which the failed central assertion is not needed.

### Chapter 14 misidentifies a distinguished pole

In §14.1.2, pp. 528–529, the point c₀ is said to be the unique point on the component allowed to be a pole of order greater than one of the finite partial products with all archimedean places included. Take F=Q(√5), χ=1 and S consisting of the two real places plus both places over 11. The prime splits (the discriminant is 5 and 4²=5 mod 11). The two finite Euler factors give (1−11^(−s))^(−2). At every 2πik/log 11 with k≠0 both real gamma factors are holomorphic and nonzero, so these are all double poles. The threshold “higher than 1” is important: the more carefully stated bound in Theorem 7.1.3.2(3)(a), p. 324, concerns pole order *exceeding [F:Q]*, and survives this example. A norm section independently defines 1/2−ix₀, so this does not disprove the section-defined moment condition or settle the conjectured L-zero multiplicities. Chapter 14 also labels its high-central-multiplicity Mordell example conditional on a rank bound, pp. 531–532. It cannot serve as an unconditional source of central zeros.

### The Artin–Weil product depends on the extension used in its definition

Chapter 14, p. 527, (14.1.1.7–9), multiplies the local factors over *the places of E* for a representation of the relative Weil group W(E/F), then asserts independence from a choice of finite Galois extension through which a representation of W(F) factors. Start with the one-dimensional trivial representation of W(Q) and take E=Q or E=Q(√5). It factors through both relative groups. At any finite place v of E the inertia-fixed line is the whole line and c_v=1 in (14.1.1.7), giving (1−(Nv)^(−s))^(−1). At each real place the trivial real factor is Γ_R(s)=π^(−s/2)Γ(s/2). Both archimedean places of Q(√5) are real, and each lies over the real place of Q. Thus (14.1.1.8) assigns Γ_R(s)ζ(s) in the first case and Γ_R(s)²ζ_E(s) in the second. On the positive real axis both Dedekind Dirichlet series tend to one as s→∞, while Γ_R(s)→∞ by Stirling's formula. They cannot be identical. Any conventional discriminant factor of exponential size leaves this Stirling comparison decisive. Already the rational prime 11 supplies two identical local factors in the second product. The usual definition takes the product over the places of F, evaluates base Frobenius on the inertia-fixed subspace, and has the required inflation property. This contradicts the paper's displayed construction of the auxiliary L-function. It does not contradict standard Artin formalism or the independent Hecke definitions in earlier chapters.

Two additional local checks explain the role of the inertia-fixed subspace. Proposition 14.1.1.1(1), p. 525, calls every relative Weil group a *central* extension. For E/F=C/R the usual W_R contains an element j with j z j^(−1)=conjugate(z) for z∈C^×, so its kernel C^× is noncentral. Further, p. 526 declares completely reducible local representations with semisimple elements to be direct sums of one-dimensional representations of the relative local Weil group. Over Q₂ the splitting field of x³−2 has Galois group S₃. The Eisenstein polynomial gives a cubic extension and adjoining a primitive cube root of unity adds degree two (the unramified quadratic cannot lie in a degree-three extension). The finite group quotient therefore supplies its irreducible two-dimensional standard representation of the relative Weil group. All image matrices have finite order and are semisimple, and the representation has no one-dimensional summand. Its inertia contains the order-three subgroup and its inertia-fixed subspace is zero. This last observation matters because the product immediately after the invariants discussion in (14.1.1.7) is indexed up to dim(V) even when the smaller invariant subspace has dimension zero. The correct Euler factor for the example is the empty product. These are local corrections to the same auxiliary construction.

### The rank-one case of the proposed Langlands normalization

Conjecture 14.1.2.2, p. 530, permits every irreducible, continuous, finite-dimensional complex Weil representation whose elements act semisimply. Equation (14.1.2.1) takes its Artin–Weil L-function on the left with the manuscript’s stated normalization and the *unitarily normalized* cuspidal L-function on the right. The text immediately below reiterates that the left side need not be unitarily normalized. Over Q take the one-dimensional norm representation ρ=|·|. On p. 528, after (14.1.1.13), the manuscript gives L(s,|·|^zρ₀)=L(s+z,ρ₀). Here the left side is Λ(s+1), where Λ(s)=π^(−s/2)Γ(s/2)ζ(s), and it has a pole at s=−1. The right side must be attached to a unitarily normalized GL₁ Hecke character. Its completed L-function is entire except for pure norm twists, for which the poles have real parts 0 and 1. No such right side has a pole at −1. Restricting the rank-one input to unitary characters, or tracking the real twist on both sides, removes this obstruction. This concerns equation (14.1.2.1) in Conjecture 14.1.2.2 as presented in Chapter 14 for a conditional multiplicity comparison. It supplies no counterexample to unitary global class field theory or any zero-location statement.

## Repaired results and false alarms

* The pointwise multiplier rule at every convergent signed-average point in Corollary A.5.4.3, pp. 585–586, fails even on R for f=sgn(x)|x|^(−1/4), ρ(x)=sgn(x)|x|^(1/4), with even compact cutoffs identically one near 0. The symmetric averages of f equal zero at 0 whereas the product averages equal one there. Taking the rank-one kernel f(x)g(y), with continuous compact g identically one near zero, makes the pointwise-for-every-regular-point argument after (12.1.1.13), p. 501, fail for a trace-class kernel at (0,0). The factors are in L² and the counterexample is at a null subset of the diagonal. The known Appendix A objection appears as I46 in [Lean ERRATA](https://github.com/mccorvie/riemann-masullo/blob/main/ERRATA.md).
* The integrated trace consequence in Theorem 12.1.1.2 is repairable. Write a nuclear kernel k=Σ s_j u_j(x) conjugate(v_j(y)) with s_j summable and u_j,v_j unit vectors. On the diagonal, smoothing by a product average gives Σ s_j A_r u_j(x) conjugate(A_r v_j(x)); interchange for fixed relatively compact window follows by Cauchy–Schwarz and nuclear convergence in L² of the product window. Almost everywhere, differentiation of all factors and domination by Σ s_j M u_j M v_j give the limit, since this majorant is integrable by the L² maximal theorem. For a continuous positive weight f and λ=fμ, localize to compact neighborhoods where f and its reciprocal are bounded, so factor norms for Haar averages are controlled by the weighted norms. On each compact region this majorizes the averages of absolute values as well, giving multiplication by a continuous factor at almost every point along the diagonal. Integration and Cauchy–Schwarz relative to λ identify the nuclear trace and its multiplier version. This proof also gives the L¹ bound for the diagonal of the difference of two trace-class kernels. It does not justify tracing arbitrary Hilbert–Schmidt kernels solely from their L² classes.
* The displayed use of individual eigenspace projections P_m T P_m→T on p. 500 is false: T on l² with eigenvalues 2^(−m), m≥1, has P_m T P_m→0 in trace norm. Cumulative projections or partial sums of the positive expansion give the needed convergence. Lemma C.3.2.2, pp. 695–696, can be verified directly: for A₀=Σ λ_j l_j⊗x_j, the traces of TA₀ and A₀T equal Σ λ_j l_j(Tx_j), using the approximation property stipulated for each of the two spaces. These facts do not equate differently ordered kernels after spatial cutoffs.
* Lemma D.3.1.2 (pp. 799–800) and the single-isotype Mellin model are compatible with the vertical-strip estimates used for Gaussian test functions in the published audit. Definition D.3.1.3 literally requests *positive* rational abscissae even for intervals wholly in the negative half-line. Use arbitrary rational abscissae exhausting the open interval's ends instead. Prior I77 in the same public Lean errata. This correction gives no evidence against Mellin inversion or the full boundary-free Schwartz transform model.
* Proposition B.5.3.2, p. 632, requires T₀≥0 and T₀≤0 and at once a uniform bound on the product of reciprocals at T₀. For χ=1 and S={∞,2} the required factor (1−2^(−iT₀))^(−1) has a pole. Dropping k=0 repairs this example. More generally, apply recurrence of the finite phase vector in its compact torus closure to a translate at which all factors are nonzero. The inverse image of an open neighborhood of that translate meets every sufficiently long interval, by compactness and relative density of the orbit. This gives good heights in both directions with linear growth and bounded gaps. The missing k=0 height does not obstruct a correctly chosen sequence of large contours.

## Checks, prior art and boundaries

The arithmetic and analytic proofs above are symbolic. None of the statements in this note certifies the entirety of an appendix. ERRATA F17, I46, I77 and I96 explicitly precede this local pass. The new higher-strength test in the contour case is presented as an example of an acknowledged hypothesis defect. Temporal priority for the stronger examples or the two other Appendix B corrections has not been established.

Independent check of Theorem 7.4.1.1(2), pp. 366–367: the selected primal vectors are explicitly a subset of the full biorthogonal family of Theorem 7.1.4.2(3), pp. 326–327. At a multiple zero ρ, all value-only selected vectors annihilate the continuous first-derivative functional, as does their closed span. With a=0, χ=1 and b(x)=e^(−ρx)h′(x) for a norm-log bump with ∫h=1, its Mellin transform Φ has Φ(ρ)=0 and Φ′(ρ)=−1 up to the positive angular mass. Multiplication sends ψ_{ρ,0} to a vector with first derivative Φ′(ρ)≠0. A compressed operator or the genuine quotient by the kernel of the low jets is a different construction. The arithmetic field with a repeated zero is given in the main report. The manuscript itself (p. 366) presents Chapter 10 as a weaker sufficient route, so this example does not alone break every proposed route.

The §3.4 digest and its merged pull request explicitly recognize the Lebesgue-integral failure over Q. The quadratic-character unordered-net example adds an illustration to that same issue.
