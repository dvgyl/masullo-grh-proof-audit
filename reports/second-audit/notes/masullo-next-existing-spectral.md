Technical note, 21 November 2026. Checked against the 940-page manuscript of 20 November, SHA-256 `e039d3f6cf0a4b14f03c7edb85097752dd65c0a2fe543c76fe992be234f97bf5`. This note revisits the functional-analytic objections in the existing public report and gives a stronger quantitative estimate for actual Riemann-zeta evaluation coordinates. ## 1. An unconditional polynomial lower bound in actual spectral interpolation

**Proposition.** Fix a strip of positive half-width $\alpha$, Sobolev degree $s=2$, the rational radial component, and the intended central parameter $a=1/2$. Let $N_0^*(T)$ count positive ordinates of simple critical-line zeros through $T$. If $f_j$ are lifts of the advertised cardinal vectors in (10.4.2.23), so their Mellin evaluations are $\delta_{jk}$ on the simple critical-line zeros $1/2+i\gamma_k$, then
$$
 \max_{0<\gamma_j\le T}\|f_j\|_{H_{\alpha,2}}
       \;\ge c_\alpha T^{\kappa_\alpha}
$$
for all sufficiently large $T$, with $c_\alpha,\kappa_\alpha>0$. One may take $\kappa_\alpha=\alpha\log(2)/(48\pi)$ after adjusting the threshold and prefactor. This is a necessary condition on *any* such lifts, however chosen, including nonsmooth lifts; it does not assert all cardinal vectors exist.

**Proof.** The norm in (9.2.2.41), p. 417, has density $w(x)=e^{2\alpha|x|}(1+x^2)^{s/2}$ in $x=\log|g|$. For $R=\alpha/2$ and $|\Re z|\le R$,
$$
 |F_j(z)|=\left|\int f_j(x)e^{zx}dx\right|
 \le C_R\|f_j\|,qquad C_R^2=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-2(\alpha-R)|x|}(1+x^2)^{-1}dx<\infty .
$$
The same bound with powers of $x$ proves holomorphy inside $|\Re z|<\alpha$. If lifts have other compact-character components, averaging over the norm-one group is a contraction and leaves the stated evaluations unchanged.

Conrey's theorem and Riemann--von Mangoldt give $N_0^*(T)\ge (6\pi)^{-1}T\log T$ for sufficiently large $T$. Indeed the proportion of simple critical-line zeros is at least $2/5$ asymptotically from below, and $N(T)\sim T\log(T)/(2\pi)$. Divide $[0,T]$ into at most $2T/L$ intervals of length at most $L=R/2=\alpha/4$, for large $T$. One interval contains $k\ge \alpha\log T/(48\pi)$ such distinct ordinates. Choose any $j$ in that interval. The disk of radius $R$ about $i\gamma_j$ lies in the Mellin domain and its inner disk of radius $R/2$ contains all other $k-1$ nodes in the interval. For a node $i\gamma_l$, set $a_l=i(\gamma_l-\gamma_j)/R$. Divide $F_j(i\gamma_j+R\zeta)$ by the finite product of disk factors $(\zeta-a_l)/(1-\overline a_l\zeta)$ over these $l\ne j$. The quotients extend holomorphically across the nodes and the factors have unit modulus on the boundary. At the center the maximum principle therefore gives
$$
 1=|F_j(i\gamma_j)|\le C_R\|f_j\|\prod_{l\ne j}|a_l|
 \le C_R\|f_j\|2^{-(k-1)}.
$$
This proves the asserted inequality, with $c_\alpha=1/(2C_R)$. There is no lower bound assumption on the zero gaps and the ordinate of a zero need not be computed. The conjugate convention for the pairing replaces all ordinates by their negatives and gives the identical disks. The proof applies about any fixed real evaluation exponent $d$ with $|d|<\alpha$ upon choosing $R<(\alpha-|d|)$.

**Significance.** The published audit deduces an unbounded, at least logarithmic subsequence from a single near pair. The many-node bound rules out even a height-uniform polylogarithmic substitute for (10.4.2.24), p. 463. It leaves open estimates allowing powers of the height; powers can be absorbed by vertical Schwartz decay for smooth tests. It does not disprove nuclearity, the uncut scalar trace, or any statement about zeta-zero locations. The underlying near-pair problem was already stated in the public discussion; the disk bound here is a strengthening under exactly the arithmetic hypotheses. No claim of priority. [Conrey's original paper](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1989.399.1) · [Earlier two-point objection](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/11/20/unforced-euler-singularity-unforced-navier-stokes-singularity-and-the-generalized-riemann-hypothesis/#comment-699655)

## 2. Chapter 9 findings and proposed repairs

**Zero counting, Lemma 9.3.2.4, p. 425.** For $G=\mathbb R_{>0}$ and the trivial character the space in log coordinates consists of the $f$ such that $e^{ax}f$ is Schwartz for each real $a$. The transform $D=e^{z^2}\sin(z^2)$ is the difference of two transforms $e^{(1\pm i)z^2}$ divided by $2i$. Their inverse Gaussians have strictly positive real decay. The imaginary zeros $i\sqrt{n\pi}$ with ordinate in $(T,T+1]$ number $\lfloor(T+1)^2/\pi\rfloor-\lfloor T^2/\pi\rfloor=2T/\pi+O(1)$. If every zero must be in the given strip, use $e^{z^2}\prod(1+z^2/n^{4/3})$. Its logarithmic growth before the Gaussian is bounded by $C|z|^{3/2}$ by the decreasing-summand integral test, so contour shifts and Cauchy estimates put its inverse in the same space. The positive count on $(T,T+1]$ is exactly $\lfloor(T+1)^{3/2}\rfloor-\lfloor T^{3/2}\rfloor$. Moving an endpoint to include equality changes the lower bound by at most one. The missing Jensen ingredient is a lower bound at the moving center. Arithmetic zero counts can replace this use for specific actual $L$-functions.

**Algebraic principal ideal, Lemma 9.3.2.5, pp. 425–426.** The literal quotient is by the image of multiplication (convolution), without a closure. For $D=ze^{2z^2}, B=ze^{z^2}$ the quotient $B/D=e^{-z^2}$ is unbounded on the imaginary axis while an integrable inverse has bounded transform. Thus $B$ is nonzero modulo the algebraic ideal despite vanishing at its only simple node. The claimed closure repair works for this particular $B$: use $H_n=e^{z^2}\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-2z^2)^j/j!$. On $|\Re z|\le A$, $|D H_n|\le |z|e^{5A^2-(\Im z)^2}$. Compact convergence and these uniform tails imply convergence in every Schwartz seminorm on each strip, also for derivatives by Cauchy. Fourier inversion on translated vertical lines gives convergence in the underlying test topology.

**Closed ideal, same lemma.** Take $D=e^{z^2}\prod_{n\ge1}(1+z^2/n^2)(1+z^2/(n+e^{-n^3})^2)$. Normal convergence and the bound $(\sinh(\pi|z|)/(\pi |z|))^2$ for the product give invertibility to an admissible test by the same strip argument. Here “invertibility” means inverse Mellin transformation; division in the algebra is not assumed. All nodes are simple and imaginary; each unit-height interval contains only boundedly many. Set $v_n=in,w_n=i(n+e^{-n^3})$. For any test transform $F$, the seminorm $p(F)=\sup_n |F(v_n)-F(w_n)|e^{n^3}$ is bounded by $\sup_y|F'(iy)|\le\int |x f(x)|dx$. It descends even through the closed ideal, all of whose elements vanish at every node. The cardinal vector at $v_n$ must have $p\ge e^{n^3}$. With target $e^{z^2}$, its term thus has $p\ge e^{n^3-n^2}$ and cannot tend to zero. The argument would still apply to the full closed vanishing ideal. Every individual node admits a cardinal test $D/((z-v_n)D'(v_n))$. The result distinguishes simultaneous Schauder expansion from individual interpolation. It says nothing about the actual Hecke zero gaps.

**Decreasing projections, Thm. 9.3.2.6, (9.3.2.10), p. 426.** Taking $D=ze^{z^2}$, $I=[-1,1]$, $s=2$ yields exactly one defining constraint $\delta_0$ for every $T>0$. It is continuous by reciprocal-weight integration, nonzero since a positive Gaussian has positive integral. Thus all projections equal the same proper hyperplane projection. For any target with evaluation one the error is bounded below by $1/\|\delta_0\|$. The general decreasing-net limit is the projection onto the intersection, and complements converge to projection onto the closed span of the node representatives. Density of their range on a proposed quotient must be proved separately. The manuscript invokes the literal theorem in the proof of 10.2.2.1 on p. 446.

## 3. A scalar trace contradiction at Sobolev degree three

**The claim tested.** Theorem 10.3.1.1(2), (10.3.1.2–4), pp. 448–449 claims trace class and a scalar trace with multiplicity $N_s(\rho)=\min(N(\rho),\lfloor(s-1)/2\rfloor+1)$ on the line $\Re z=a$ for all $s>1$. At $s=3$ the formula counts a double zero twice. The following test proves the scalar formula itself is false. It also independently establishes trace class for the operator used in this test. The example uses a zero-width line $I=\{0\}$ and comes with no conclusion about the positive-width Chapter 12 trace.

### Arithmetic field and a positive test

Set $F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2,\zeta_3)$ and $K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)$. Eisenstein and the discriminant $-108$ give $\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)=S_3$ and $\operatorname{Gal}(F/K)=C_3$. For the two inverse nontrivial finite-order Hecke characters $\psi,\bar\psi$ of this cyclic extension, complex conjugation of $K$ interchanges the two characters and relabels the ideals of each fixed norm, including their conductor exclusions. Hence the two $L$-functions are equal. Abelian factorization gives $\zeta_F=\zeta_K L_K(\cdot,\psi)^2$. The Hecke zero count supplies a nontrivial zero $a+i\gamma$ of $L_K(\cdot,\psi)$ with $0<a<1$ and finite nonzero ordinate. As $\zeta_K$ is holomorphic there, the order for $\zeta_F$ is at least two. One may specify the least positive ordinate of such a zero and, to break ties, the smallest real part. Actual numerical values and any location conjecture are unnecessary.

Choose $v\in C_c^\infty((1,2))$ real with $\int v=1$. Use log-Haar $dx$, the transform convention $\widehat b(\xi)=\int b(x)e^{i\xi x}dx$, and $b(x)=e^{-i\gamma x}v(x)$. Write $b^*(x)=\overline{b(-x)}$, $\varphi=b*b^*$ and $B=\widehat\varphi=|\widehat b|^2$. Thus $B\ge0$, $B(\gamma)=1$, and $B$ is Schwartz. Put $V=\bar\mu_1(C_F^1)>0$ and choose the adelic test $\phi(g)=V^{-1}\varphi(\log|g|)$. Positive constant changes from the norm section are thereby removed. Theorem 10.3.1.1 imposes no restriction on the sign, log-support, or value at the identity of $\phi$. Choose base representatives trivial on the norm section. Integrating over the compact group $C_F^1$ projects onto radial vectors, annihilating every other compact character both on the Hilbert quotient and in the printed trace. In particular our comparison is unaffected by the Chapter 12 volume issue.

### Fourier description of the annihilator, using only one generator

Up to a common Hilbert norm factor $V^{1/2}$, the ambient radial space is $H=L^2(\mathbb R,(1+x^2)^{3/2}dx)$. The Fourier transform in $x$ identifies it with ordinary $H^{3/2}(\mathbb R_\xi)$. Denote by $M$ the closed radial geometric space, generated by the $|g|^a$-twisted polar-free theta sums. The Hilbert anti-dual of $H/M$ is an invariant closed subspace $E$ in $H^{-3/2}(\mathbb R_\xi)$, the annihilator of $\widehat M$. Scalar product conjugations below may be chosen so that annihilation means a distribution acting linearly on a test; equivalently conjugate both its argument and multiplier. Our multiplier is real.

There is a radial generator $h\in M$ with $\widehat h(\xi)$ nonzero except at the distinct ordinates $Z=\{\xi:\zeta_F(a+i\xi)=0\}$. To see this take $g$ the product of the standard Gaussian at all archimedean places and $1_{\widehat{\mathcal O}_F}$ at the finite places. Its unfolded zeta integral is the completed Dedekind zeta function times a nonvanishing entire exponential and a nonzero constant, as follows directly from the local unramified Euler integrals and archimedean Gamma integrals. For an idèle of norm $c>1$ write $R_cg(y)=g(j_c y)$. Put $f=(1-R_c)(1-cR_c)g$. The first difference kills the value at zero, and the second kills the additive integral because the latter scales by $c^{-1}$. Thus $f$ is polar-free in the two global senses defining $S_0(\mathbb A_F)$; it need not be flat to infinite order, a condition from a different space. Average $|g'|^a\Theta_f(g')$ over $C_F^1$ to obtain $h$. Poisson summation gives rapid decay at both ends, including after multiplication by any fixed exponential in $x$. Unfolding in $\Re z>1$ and continuing yields, up to nonzero factors,
$$
 \widehat h(\xi)=(1-c^{-(a+i\xi)})(1-c^{1-(a+i\xi)})\Lambda_F(a+i\xi).
$$
Both additional factors are nonzero on this line. All translates of $h$ lie in $M$, since translation of a theta generator is induced by idelic dilation and a scalar, preserving both global moment conditions. For $U\in E$, annihilation of these translates implies $\widehat h\,U=0$ as distributions. Indeed test against an arbitrary compactly supported smooth multiplier by Fourier inversion into translates; the coefficients decrease rapidly and the Hilbert norms of the translated generator grow only polynomially. With sesquilinear pairing this is $\overline{\widehat h}\,U=0$, giving the same real zero set. Dividing by the smooth nonzero factor on any compact interval avoiding $Z$ proves $\operatorname{supp}U\subseteq Z$. One does not need equality with the full space of $Z$-supported distributions, any basis theorem, or spectral synthesis.

If $U$ has finite support, localize to each point using smooth cutoffs bounded on $H^{-3/2}$. A distribution supported at one point is a finite sum of delta derivatives. The inverse Fourier transform is a polynomial times a complex exponential. The $H^{-3/2}$ norm is finite only for a constant polynomial: for degree one its squared norm has a $1/|x|$ tail, and higher degrees diverge faster. Thus on a set of $k$ points the allowable distributions have dimension at most $k$, even if some zeta zeros have higher multiplicity. This argument localizes before testing integrability, excluding a possible cancellation between distinct atoms.

### Trace class and spectral counting

On $E$, the dual of the quotient operator acts by the real multiplier $B$, because convolution preserves $M$. Independently of Theorem 10.3.1.1 it is trace class there. Indeed choose a compactly supported $C^\infty$ partition of unity $\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}\chi(\xi-j)=1$ with supports in $[j-1,j+1]$. Set $m_j=B\chi(\cdot-j)$. The multiplier $m_j$ on $H^{-3/2}$ has norm $O_N((1+|j|)^{-N})$ for every $N$. To verify, Fourier-transform in $\xi$ and use the moderate weight inequality
$$
 \langle x\rangle^{-3/2}\le 2^{3/4}\langle x-y\rangle^{3/2}\langle y\rangle^{-3/2}.
$$
Young's inequality bounds the operator norm by a constant times $\int |\check m_j(t)|\langle t\rangle^{3/2}dt$. Smooth compact support on the interval of length two and four integrations by parts give $|\check m_j(t)|\ll_N (1+|j|)^{-N}(1+|t|)^{-4}$. On $E$ the image is supported on $Z\cap [j-1,j+1]$. By the point-mass calculation its rank is at most the cardinality of that set. The general fixed-field Dedekind zero count $O_F((|j|+2)\log(|j|+2))$ suffices. Even if the individual multipliers had failed to preserve $E$, orthogonally projecting the outputs back to $E$ preserves these bounds. Rank times operator norm bounds trace norm. The sum therefore converges in trace norm, and its action is exactly $B|_E$ by the partition of unity and closedness/invariance. Compact-character orthogonality then gives trace class for the full quotient operator of this radial test.

For each nonzero eigenvalue $\lambda$ and every generalized eigenvector $U$ of the compact operator $B|_E$, we have $(B-\lambda)^kU=0$ for some integer $k$. Consequently $U$ is supported on $Z\cap\{B=\lambda\}$. This set is finite because $B$ tends to zero and $Z$ is locally finite. If it is nonempty, $\lambda$ is real and positive. The generalized eigenspace has dimension at most the number of distinct ordinates in this fiber, and all its elements are actual eigenvectors since they contain only point masses. Lidskii's trace formula yields
$$
   \operatorname{Tr}A(\phi)\le \sum_{\xi\in Z} B(\xi).
$$
The trace of the dual is the conjugate of the primal trace; all the nonzero eigenvalues here are nonnegative, so conjugation has no effect. In contrast (10.3.1.3) gives
$$
   \sum_{\xi\in Z} \min\{N(a+i\xi),2\} B(\xi)
       \ \ge\ 1+\sum_{\xi\in Z} B(\xi).
$$
All series converge absolutely by the same zero count and Schwartz decay. A multiplicity at some other point cannot compensate for this strict excess because the test is nonnegative in frequency. This proves a failure of the **scalar formula** at an allowed Sobolev degree even after granting, or proving, trace class.

**Repair and scope.** At the threshold $s=3$ replace the coefficient count by the one for $2m+1<s$. In the proof above that gives the required upper bound; establishing a corresponding lower bound would additionally show every individual evaluation survives the quotient. Tate's common-factor statement on the radial theta ideal would provide that separate step. This correction addresses one line and does not automatically establish any general-window identity. The source of the defect, the strict endpoint inequality, is public. The proof of a strict scalar discrepancy by Fourier support, trace-class localization, and a positive test is a separate argument. [Previous endpoint objection](https://github.com/mccorvie/riemann-masullo/issues/2469#issuecomment-7057757662).

**Exact value for this test.** The lower bound required for equality in the true trace can in fact be supplied at $0<a<1$. For any additive Schwartz--Bruhat seed yielding a polar-free theta image, Mellin evaluation on the trivial component unfolds for $\Re z>1$ into a finite sum of products of local zeta integrals. Outside a finite set of places the factor is the usual Euler factor. At each exceptional finite place, averaging over units leaves finitely many exceptional valuation shells and an eventually constant tail, so multiplication by $1-q_v^{-z}$ leaves a Laurent polynomial in $q_v^{-z}$. The archimedean integrals are holomorphic for $\Re z>0$, by additive smoothness near zero and rapid decay at infinity. Thus the unfolded expression is $\zeta_F(z)$ times a function holomorphic near $a+i\xi$ (identities continued from $\Re z>1$). Polar-free cancellation makes the evaluation of the theta Fourier integral well-defined there. This proves each point evaluation at $\xi\in Z$ annihilates all geometric generators. Evaluation is continuous in $H^{3/2}$ by Fourier Cauchy--Schwarz and extends to their closure. Hence every $\delta_\xi$ belongs to $E$, and the preceding upper bound on the dimension of each nonzero spectral fiber is attained. In particular the true trace is exactly $\sum_{\xi\in Z}B(\xi)$, whereas the printed excess is exactly $\sum_{N(a+i\xi)\ge2}B(\xi)\ge1$ for the chosen normalization.

### A concrete central field and a test supported to the right of one

There is a stronger version with *both* the ordinate and the center specified: put
$$
 K_8=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5,\sqrt{21}),\quad m=\frac{5+\sqrt5}{2}\frac{21+\sqrt{21}}2,
 \qquad F_8=K_8(\sqrt{-3m}).
$$
The subscript is a label; $K_8$ has degree four. Martinet proves in Example 2 of his 1971 paper (p. 407) that $F_8/\mathbb Q$ is a tamely ramified quaternion Galois extension of degree eight and its integers are not free over $\mathbb Z[Q_8]$. Fröhlich's theorem for tame quaternion extensions then gives a negative root number for the degree-two irreducible symplectic character and $\zeta_{F_8}(1/2)=0$. The field's completed zeta is invariant under $s\mapsto 1-s$ and its Gamma factors are finite and nonzero at $1/2$. Consequently this central zero has even order, at least two. This argument uses no floating-point evaluations. Martinet's Example 1, with the positive radicand $m$, has a free integer ring; the negative radicand $-3m$ in Example 2 is essential here. [Martinet, *Modules sur l'algèbre du groupe quaternionien*, Examples 1–2](https://www.numdam.org/article/ASENS_1971_4_4_3_399_0.pdf) · [Fröhlich, *Artin-root numbers and normal integral bases for quaternion fields*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01418937) · [Martinet, *Fonctions zêta au point 1/2*, quaternion theorem and corollary on pp. 114–115](https://www.numdam.org/item/STNG_1971-1972__1__108_0.pdf)

Take $F=F_8$, $a=1/2$, $\gamma=0$ in the preceding proof. There is now literal overlap with Connes's original Theorem 1 and Corollary 2, §III, pp. 13–14, which give the strict multiplicity rule and trace at the central line. This supplies a second check, independent of the preceding distribution argument. The corrected cap in that setting is historical. [Connes's preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9811068)

The trace discrepancy persists for a whole family of one-sided tests. Let $v\ge0$ be smooth with integral one and support in $(1,2)$, and put $\phi_L(g)=V^{-1}L^{-1}v(\log|g|/L)$ for positive integers $L$. The multiplier $B_L(\xi)=\widehat v(L\xi)$ has $B_L(0)=1$. The proof with finite fibers also gives the exact trace for complex Schwartz multipliers, with conjugation restored on taking the primal trace. Alternatively apply Connes's Corollary 2 directly. Thus the discrepancy (printed minus actual) equals $\sum_{\xi\in Z:\,N(1/2+i\xi)\ge2}B_L(\xi)$. At nonzero ordinates the terms tend to zero and for all $L\ge1$ they admit a summable majorant by rapid decay and the zero count. The discrepancy tends to one. It is real by conjugate symmetry, hence exceeds $1/2$ for all sufficiently large $L$. Every such test is supported at norms in $(e^L,e^{2L})$.

## 4. Positive-width trace-class and trace assertions are incompatible at an actual strip edge

**Proposition.** In Theorem 10.3.2.1(2), pp. 453–454, (10.3.2.2–4), choose $F=F_8$, $a=\alpha=1/4$, $s=3$. There is a fixed radial smooth test whose positive convolution powers cannot all have the trace prescribed by the theorem if its asserted trace-class property holds. Under trace class, all sufficiently large powers fail. This uses a positive strip and explicitly accounts for the domain of its weighted Hilbert-space adjoint. *It does not assert independent trace class for the positive-width operator.*

Use the same nonnegative log bump $v$ of mass one, support in $(1,2)$, positive on some interval. Set $\phi(g)=V^{-1}v(\log|g|)$, $B(z)=\int v(y)e^{zy}dy$, $R=B(\alpha)>0$. For every $b\in[-\alpha,\alpha]$, $|B(b+i\xi)|\le R$ with equality only for $(b,\xi)=(\alpha,0)$. For $b<\alpha$ positivity of the log support gives strict inequality already without oscillation. At the upper edge the triangle inequality is strict for $\xi\ne0$ because the phase is nonconstant on an interval of positive density. The target at the upper edge is exactly the central multiple zero $a+\alpha=1/2$. Its printed weight in (10.3.2.4) equals two.

Denote the radial quotient convolution operator by $T$. Its powers act by $\phi^{*n}$, an admissible test supported at positive log norms in $(n,2n)$. Write $w=e^{2\alpha|x|}(1+x^2)^{3/2}$. For $y\ge0$ the weight ratio gives
$$
 \|T_y\|_{L^2(w)}\le C e^{\alpha y}(1+y)^{3/2},
$$
also on the quotient by the invariant geometric closure. Integrating against $v^{*n}$ shows $\|T^n\|\le C(1+2n)^{3/2}R^n$, so $r(T)\le R$. The full compact-character decomposition reduces the test to this radial quotient.

**Boundary eigenspace.** Embed the continuous linear dual of the quotient in $L^2(w^{-1}dx)$ by the ordinary pairing $f\mapsto\int f\eta$. The transpose convolution is $\eta\mapsto\int v(y)\eta(x+y)dy$. Suppose a generalized eigenvector belongs to an eigenvalue $\lambda$ of modulus $R$. Write $\eta(x)=e^{\alpha x}g(x)$ and $\omega=\lambda/R$. Then $(P-\omega)^k g=0$ for some $k\ge1$, where
$$
 Pg(x)=\int g(x+y)\,d\nu(y),\qquad d\nu(y)=R^{-1}v(y)e^{\alpha y}dy.
$$
This is a probability law supported in $[1,2]$. The function $g$ is locally integrable, since $\eta$ is locally square integrable. Expanding the equation expresses $g$ as a finite sum of smooth convolutions $P^jg$, $j\ge1$, and gives a smooth representative satisfying the equation pointwise.

On $x\ge0$, $g\in L^2((1+x^2)^{-3/2}dx)$. We verify that the negative tail also has tempered growth. For $k=1$, iterate $g=\omega^{-1}Pg$ along independent steps of law $\nu$, stopping when the increasing walk first reaches $[0,\infty)$ from $x<0$. The stopping time is bounded by $\lceil -x\rceil$ and the overshoot lies in $[0,2]$, so $|g(x)|\le\max_{[0,2]}|g|$. More generally set $h=(P-\omega)g$ and iterate $g=\omega^{-1}(Pg-h)$. The resulting inhomogeneous sum has at most $\lceil-x\rceil$ terms over points in $[x,2]$, each multiplied by a scalar of modulus one. By induction $|h(t)|\le C(1+|t|)^{k-2}$ there. Thus $|g(x)|\le C'(1+|x|)^{k-1}$ on $x<0$. This proof is a finite iteration and uses no interchange at an unbounded stopping time.

These bounds make $g$ a tempered distribution over the whole line. Its Fourier transform is supported where the Fourier multiplier of $P$ equals $\omega$ on the real axis. A continuous, nonlattice positive probability density has Fourier transform of modulus less than one off zero (strict triangle inequality), and its value at zero is one. For $\omega\ne1$ this forces $g=0$. For $\omega=1$, $\widehat g$ is supported at the origin, hence $g$ is a polynomial. Its positive-tail norm excludes every nonconstant polynomial, again by the logarithmic divergence at degree one. It follows that the entire generalized eigenspace on the peripheral circle is at most one-dimensional and belongs to $\lambda=R$. This conclusion holds even for the ambient dual, without first identifying the actual arithmetic annihilator. For $\lambda\ne0$ compact operators and their transposes have the same algebraic multiplicities, with complex conjugation if using a Hilbert adjoint.

**Incompatible normalized moments.** If $T$ is trace class, its eigenvalues are absolutely summable with algebraic multiplicities. The spectral-radius bound, the peripheral result, and Lidskii's theorem give
$$
 \lim_{n\to\infty}R^{-n}\operatorname{Tr}(T^n)
 =\operatorname{mult}_{\rm alg}(R,T)\le 1.
$$
The exchange of limit and sum follows by domination with $|\lambda|/R$. On the other hand the right side of (10.3.2.3) for $\phi^{*n}$ is the sum of $N_{I,s}(\rho)B(b+i\xi)^n$ over the selected zeros $\rho=a+b+i\xi$ of the trivial component. All terms except $b=\alpha,\xi=0$ tend to zero after division by $R^n$. A summable majorant independent of $n\ge1$ is $N(\rho)|B(b+i\xi)|/R$, using uniform strip decay and the Dedekind zero count. The limit of the printed right side is exactly two. The two normalized moments differ for every sufficiently large $n$ if trace class holds. This disproves the asserted conjunction at a positive width. A strict edge cap makes these two asymptotic limits compatible. None of this calculation moves the actual central zero to the edge of an interval centered at $1/2$; the offset $a=1/4$ is essential.

**Scope of novelty.** The two-sided Sobolev integrability calculation and the corrected cutoff are old. The arithmetic and random-walk arguments show how the mistaken cap becomes a scalar discrepancy with an explicit degree-eight field, logarithmic test bump, and strip center/width. No priority claim follows. In particular this is an objection to a published-range intermediate theorem, rather than a demonstration of an off-line zero, a counterexample to the main formula at the particular positive windows centered at $1/2$, or a disproof of GRH.

## 5. Independent checks of the remaining operator findings

**(10.4.3.45), Theorem 10.4.3.5, pp. 472–473.** Here is a rederivation of the concrete test in the existing audit. Take the trivial rational component, $a=1/2$, $s=4$, $w=e^{2\alpha|x|}(1+x^2)^2$, $x=\log |g|$ and $\alpha\ge0$. Normalize the compact factor to mass one. Let $P$ be the orthogonal projection onto the orthogonal complement of the closed twisted theta image $M$, and $G=1-P$. For an actual real ordinate $\gamma$ with $\zeta(1/2+i\gamma)=0$, put $u=e^{-i\gamma x}/w$. Then $u\in L^2(wdx)$ and $\langle m,u\rangle=0$ for $m\in M$. Indeed, average the adelic finite test over the finite unit groups. The resulting test is a finite linear combination of $1_{d\widehat{\mathbb Z}}$, $d>0$ rational, by local constancy at zero and finite support away from zero in the valuation variable. The unfolded Mellin transform for each term is a factor $d^{-z}\zeta(z)$ times the Mellin transform of its two real half-lines. The latter is holomorphic for $\Re z>0$. Analytic continuation or Poisson summation on the full polar-free seed gives the vanishing asserted at $z=1/2+i\gamma$. Continuity of pairing uses $\int w^{-1}<\infty$. Thus $Pu=u$.

Take a real bump $b\ge0$, of mass one, supported in $(1,2)$, positive on an interval. In logarithmic coordinates $\phi(t)=e^{-i\gamma t}b(t)$. For the actual left convolution in (9.3.1.2), $wAu=e^{-i\gamma x}D(x)$, where $D(x)=\int b(t)w(x)/w(x-t)dt$. If $GAu=0$, this function annihilates all translates of an explicit nonzero seed $h$: take $f_\infty=q-2q(2\cdot)$ with $q$ smooth, nonzero, nonnegative, even, supported in $1<|t|<3/2$, and finite factor $1_{\widehat{\mathbb Z}}$. Then $f(0)=\int f=0$, the theta sum $h(x)=e^{x/2}\sum_{n\ne0}f_\infty(ne^x)$ is nonzero on part of $1<e^x<3/2$, is eventually zero as $x\to+\infty$, and decays faster than every exponential as $x\to-\infty$ by Poisson summation. All translates belong to $M$ since idele action rescales the twist by a constant. The entire Fourier transform of $h$ is nonzero almost everywhere on the real axis. By Plancherel and uniqueness on $L^1$, translates of $h$ span a dense subspace of ordinary $L^2(dx)$.

The difference $e^{-i\gamma x}(D(x+1)-D(x))$ therefore annihilates a dense space in ordinary $L^2$. It belongs to this space since $D(x)=\int b(t)e^{\pm2\alpha t}dt+O(1/|x|)$ uniformly at its two ends. It is zero, $D$ is periodic, and its limit at $+\infty$ forces it to be constant. When $\alpha>0$ its two limits differ. When $\alpha=0$ the limit is one and $D(0)<1/4$. Thus $GA P u\ne0$ even at zero width. Each finite lifted-kernel sum has range in $\operatorname{ran}P$ by the choice of output vectors on pp. 472–473. A pointwise or operator-norm limit with that range cannot equal $AP$ as claimed. Replacing the output with $PA$ passes this range test, but changes the kernel being subtracted in Definition 10.4.3.7. The difference does not itself fix a scalar trace after localization. Remark 10.4.1.4 already recognizes the off-diagonal block. [Prior ordering objection](https://github.com/mccorvie/riemann-masullo/issues/2469#issuecomment-7057757877)

**(11.2.1.4), (11.2.1.9), pp. 488–489.** Use the same rational test at $\alpha=0$, $S=\{\infty\}$, so class number one and the trivial character meet the stated restrictions. Both Paley–Wiener projections $Q_L$ have range in $M$, and are orthogonal, hence $Q_Lu=0$. For $T=M_\rho GA$, choose a compact interval carrying positive squared norm of $GAu$, and choose an admissible $(\Lambda,\varepsilon)$-cutoff equal to one there. Such cutoffs exist by enlarging $\Lambda$ in Definition 9.3.3.1, p. 427. For this fixed cutoff, $Tu\ne0$ and $TQ_Lu=0$ at every level. Both operator limits fail even in the strong topology. If $Q_L\to G$ strongly and $T$ is trace class, the trace-norm limit is $TG$ by approximate finite rank and the self-adjointness of $Q_L$. The witness proves no discrepancy for the traces of $T$ and $TG$ alone.

**Lemma 10.4.2.5, pp. 461–462.** The lemma explicitly assumes the prescribed quotient vectors lie in $F/F_0$, which a density-only objection would overlook. Use $F=E$, $E_0=0$ to meet this condition exactly. In the Hilbert direct sum of blocks $\mathbb C^n\oplus\mathbb C^n$, let $e_i,f_j$ be the standard vectors, $v=n^{-1/2}\sum_j f_j$, and $d=n^{-1/2}\sum_i e_i$. First list all pairs $(e_i+v,e_i)$ and then all $(f_j,f_j-d)$. Each cross lower-left pairing equals $-1/\sqrt n+1/\sqrt n=0$. All other pairings give the Kronecker deltas by orthogonality. The two families separately span each finite block and are total. Both norms are between 1 and $\sqrt2$; reciprocal rescaling makes the primal unit if desired. The quotient is identity, so totality forces the unique lifts to be the primal vectors. The block's halfway partial sum sends $d$ to $d+\sqrt n v$, whose norm is $\sqrt{1+n}$. Uniform boundedness precludes a Schauder basis. This independently verifies the published audit's objection to the Markushevich alternative even with uniformly bounded biorthogonal pairs. It preserves the individual lift and norm conclusions in this example.

**Equation (10.4.2.24), p. 463.** In addition to §1 above, the one-pair estimate follows directly from $|e^{ivx}-e^{itx}|\le|v-t|\,|x|$ and the integrability of $x^2/w$ for positive width. Conrey's positive proportion of simple critical-line zeros supplies a pair within $O(1/\log T)$ among heights up to $T$, yielding lift norm at least a constant times $\log T$. Proposition 10.2.2.2 estimates the norms of the evaluations themselves, which is consistent with their becoming arbitrarily close. Page 463 mixes $s+b$ with $a+b$ and introduces an extra $a$ in the displayed functions relative to Thm. 10.2.2.1. If the formulas on p. 463 are read literally, choose $a=s=2$, $b=-3/2$, $\alpha=2$. The zero is still on the critical line, and either real evaluation exponent $d=-3/2$ or $1/2$ lies strictly inside the strip. Both the direct one-pair estimate and the many-node disk bound hold about such a fixed $d$. An uncontrolled large basis norm need not prevent trace class. The argument does not prove the scalar cutoff identity false.

## 6. Additional check of the function spaces in Chapter 5

Remark 2.1.2.1, p. 63, includes all Schwartz functions of the discrete valuation, constant on the unit classes, in $S(F_v^\times)$. Definition 5.3.4.1 and (5.3.4.1), p. 259, include this full space in what is called a subspace of the additive Schwartz–Bruhat tests. Over $\mathbb Q_2$ put $h(2^nu)=e^{-\sqrt n}$ for $n\ge1$, $u\in\mathbb Z_2^\times$, and zero otherwise. It is smooth and rapidly decreasing in the valuation, but no choice of $h(0)$ makes it locally constant at the origin. In the invariant sector the actual intersection of the two test spaces is the multiplicative compactly supported tests: additive compactness bounds the valuation below, and additive local constancy near zero together with valuation decay forces eventual zero.

Even on an artificially enlarged additive space, the local Mellin transform on $\Re s>0$ (unit mass normalized) equals $H(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}e^{-\sqrt n}2^{-ns}$. Termwise derivatives from the right at zero exist in every order by summable domination. The absolute value of the $k$th Taylor coefficient is at least $e^{-2k}(4k^2\log 2)^k/k!$, from the single term $n=4k^2$; the $k$th root tends to infinity. Thus a holomorphic extension around zero is impossible. Tensor with a compact real bump on the positive half-line whose Mellin transform is nonzero at zero; the product has the same defect. This disproves Lemma 5.3.4.2(2) on the literal space, p. 260. A twist by $|\cdot|_2^{-1}$ gives $2^ne^{-\sqrt n}$, which fails valuation decay in (3). Restricting to the multiplicative compactly supported test factors is one possible repair of these counterexamples.

The proof on p. 260 also claims an inference from an entire product to local vanishing. Let $q$ be a smooth nonnegative nonzero bump supported in $(1,2)$, $g(x)=q(x)-q(2x)$ and $f_2=1_{\mathbb Z_2}$. Over $S=\{\infty,2\}$ their trivial compact-character zeta product is $B(s)(1-2^{-s})/(1-2^{-s})=B(s)$ with $B$ entire, yet $f_2(0)=1$. A finite character nontrivial on $\mathbb Z_2^\times$ has identically zero local factor, and the real sign character makes no change to the positive-support real Mellin transform. This is a counterexample to an inference about the displayed tensor representative, not to the claim that the same product admits a different polar-free representative: $q\otimes 1_{\mathbb Z_2^\times}$ realizes this trivial-character product.

## 7. Remaining scope

- **Established:** the invalid upper endpoint changes an actual zero-width scalar trace, and the corresponding positive-width trace-class-plus-formula assertion fails for the specified offset strip. These are stronger than disproving a jet formula alone. The polynomial lower bound on actual cardinal lifts and the quoted projection/lifting tests are independent of any conjectural zero location.
- **Unverified:** broad nuclearity on positive-width quotients, a corrected trace identity for positive-width strips centered at $1/2$, and how the established failures could propagate into a direct counterexample to a normalized final scalar identity. No such counterexample is presented.
- **Resolved false alarms:** a nonzero off-diagonal block need not change a trace. Positive width does admit all finite polynomial jets strictly inside the strip because of the exponential margin on both tails. The simple zero case of the endpoint calculation has count one at $s=3$ in both formulas. The closed-ideal example of §2 concerns the general functional-analytic lemma and imposes no closeness condition on genuine zeta zeros. The cancellation example in Chapter 5 even admits an explicit polar-free alternative representative.
- Masullo himself mentions multiple central zeros and the work of Armitage and Serre, p. 529. Nor is the strict central multiplicity rule new: Connes's theorem and trace corollary already give it. The central-field test and the offset positive-strip argument quantify the consequences for Masullo's stated formulas; the public objections examined cannot establish priority.

## 8. A degree-two example meeting the later lower bound on Sobolev degree

There is also an unconditional central multiple zero over $K=\mathbb Q(i)$ for which the tests of §§3–4 work at $s=5\ge 2[K:\mathbb Q]$. This refinement combines the independent congruent-number curve calculation in the companion trace re-audit with the peripheral multiplicity argument above.

Write $n=1254=2\cdot3\cdot11\cdot19$, $E:y^2=x^3-n^2x$. The rational points $P=(-1058,21896)$, $Q=(-912,25992)$ both satisfy the equation. For the two-isogeny descent homomorphism defined by the squareclass of $x$, with values $-n^2$ at $T_0=(0,0)$ and $1$ at the identity, the images of $T_0,T_n=(n,0),P,Q$ have classes $-1,1254,-2,-57$. These are independent by valuations at $11,19,2$ successively and then the sign. Thus $E(\mathbb Q)/2E(\mathbb Q)$ has dimension at least four. Its dimension is $\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb Q)+2$, since the rational $2$-torsion has dimension two. Hence $\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb Q)\ge 2$. For positive squarefree $n\equiv6\pmod8$ the congruent-number curve has root number $-1$, forcing odd analytic rank. The Gross–Zagier–Kolyvagin analytic-rank-one implication rules out order one, since it would give algebraic rank one. The unitarily normalized CM Hecke character $\chi$ of $K$ attached to $E$ therefore has an $L$-function with multiplicity $m\ge3$ at $1/2$. No converse to BSD in higher rank is used. Masullo's different conditional example based on Mordell curves, Proposition 14.1.2.5, pp. 531–532, invokes a separate rank inequality. USSR-Izv. 32 (1989), 523–541. The same curve is used in the earlier third-party audit of the Chapter 13 multiplicity claim. For $k\in C_K^1$, $x\in\mathbb R$, use $\phi(k\tau(e^x))=V^{-1}\overline{\chi(k)}v(x)$. The smooth test has compact support with log norm in $(1,2)$. Left convolution on $\overline{\chi(k)}f(x)$ acts by $v*f$. It kills the other compact types, by integration over the norm-one group. **The fixed exceptional point in the auxiliary simplicity conjecture.** Page 529, Conjecture 14.1.2.1(2), admits every cuspidal automorphic representation $\pi$ of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q})$ and exempts only $s=1/2$.
