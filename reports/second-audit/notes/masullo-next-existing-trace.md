## Finding beyond the published audit: the choice of norm section can expose a multiple central zero

**Precise target.** Theorem 13.3.1.1, p. 522, caps each on-line zero at the degree of the number field, restricts the tests by (13.3.1.2), and allows a choice of continuous homomorphic section of the idelic norm. Even *after* replacing all inconsistent local coefficients and global measures by the classical ones in (B.4.4.16), the resulting assertion is false for the full stated choice of section. This is an additional mathematical difficulty independent of the earlier rational archimedean-factor error. The conclusion concerns the capped formula of Chapter 13, not the uncapped Chapter 12 formula. The blank Theorem 13.3.1.2 has no formal statement to refute. The discussion on pp. 528–529 does specify which point is declared central using the section.

**Arithmetic input.** Set K = Q(i) and let E over Q be the congruent-number curve y² = x³ − n²x, n = 1254 = 2·3·11·19. The group E(Q)/2E(Q) has dimension at least four over F₂. Indeed the standard two-isogeny descent homomorphism E(Q) → Q*/Q*² sends O to 1, T₀=(0,0) to −n², and each point with nonzero x to its x coordinate modulo squares. The homomorphism follows from the divisor of x being 2(T₀)−2(O), or from the chord law: the product of the three nonzero abscissas on a nonvertical line is the square of its intercept. The exceptional value at T₀ is the coefficient −n² of the linear term in the curve equation. For T₀, Tₙ=(n,0), P=(−1058,21896) and Q=(−912,25992), the images are −1, 1254, −2 and −57. Exact substitution verifies P and Q. Prime 11 forces the coefficient of 1254 to vanish in any squareclass relation. The valuation at 19 then eliminates −57, that at 2 eliminates −2, and the sign eliminates −1. Thus the four images are independent. As E[2](Q) has dimension two, Mordell–Weil gives rank at least two.

For a positive squarefree n the congruent-number twist has sign −1 when n ≡ 5, 6, 7 (mod 8). Here n ≡ 6. The analytic rank is odd; the Gross–Zagier–Kolyvagin theorem for modular elliptic curves over Q implies that analytic rank one would give algebraic rank one. Thus the central analytic order m is at least three. This uses the proved rank-one implication, not BSD in rank three. Since E has CM by Z[i], its modular L-function is the Hecke L-function of its CM Grössencharacter over K. Normalizing unitarily gives a Hecke character χ of K whose L-function has order m ≥ 3 at s = 1/2. Its complex component has angular weight ε = +1 or −1, trivial on positive reals under the usual unitary normalization. Replacing χ by the conjugate if desired interchanges the signs and has no bearing on the construction. Classically this identification can be seen from induction of the one-dimensional CM Galois character to Q, including at bad places. Equivalently one may use Masullo's own Deuring factorization (14.1.2.7) for base change to K: E is isomorphic over Q to its quadratic twist by −1 (the quartic-twist model has coefficient multiplied by (−1)²), so base change has L-function L(E/Q,s)². Its two CM factors are exchanged by conjugation and have equal central vanishing orders.

**The section and its effect on the jets.** At the complex place use the usual local norm |z|². Take τ(t) to have archimedean component sqrt(t) exp(i log t) and all finite components one. Its image in C_K is a continuous homomorphic section of norm. Thus χ(τ(t))=t^(iω) with ω=ε ≠0. Compare §14.1.2, pp. 528–529, which locates its distinguished central point at s=1/2−iω in the χ parametrization. The actual zero just constructed remains at 1/2; changing τ does not change L(s,χ).

Take nonnegative h∈C_c^∞((1,2)) of positive integral in logarithmic coordinates, with H(z)=∫h(x)e^(zx)dx, and set b=(d/dx−iω)²h. Then B(z)=∫b(x)e^(zx)dx=(z+iω)²H(z) and B(0)=−ω²H(0)≠0. For u≥0 test with φᵤ(g)=overline(χ(g)) b(log|g|−u). It is smooth, compactly supported at norms >1. For any tested unitary character η whose compact restriction differs from χ, the inner integral in (13.3.1.2) vanishes by compact orthogonality. In the matching case η=χ |·|^(iv). As η(τ(t))=t^(i(ω+v)), inserting φᵤ into the double integral gives a positive constant times exp((s+iv)u)B(s+iv). The first two moments are its value and first derivative at s=−i(ω+v). Both vanish because B has a double zero at −iω. The published theorem imposes precisely these two orders for [K:Q]=2. Notice that imposing B(0)=B′(0)=0 instead would test the wrong section.

**Incompatibility with the classical formula.** Compare the repaired formula with the ordinary all-zero explicit formula, using identical geometric local terms, polar terms, and canonical Haar scale. For λ=ρ−1/2 in the χ component, let dλ be the full multiplicity at off-line zeros and its excess over 2 (if positive) on the line. Compact-character orthogonality on φᵤ isolates this component. If both formulas held then ∑ dλ B(λ)exp(λu)=0 for every u≥0. Its Laplace transform, initially on Re w>1/2, is ∑ dλ B(λ)/(w−λ). Repeated integration by parts gives rapid vertical decay of B uniformly in |Re λ|≤1/2. The fixed-character O(T log T) zero count gives absolute summability and locally normal convergence off the locally finite poles. At w=0 the residue is (m−2)B(0)≠0, contradicting uniqueness of meromorphic continuation. The result is independent of GRH and requires no estimate of m above three. It disproves an otherwise canonically normalized degree-capped formula, conditional only on allowing the sections explicitly permitted in (13.3.1.2).

**Limits and repairs.** For the positive real section τ₀(t)=sqrt(t), the same χ has ω=0; all these moment-null tests then kill the actual central zero. Thus restricting τ to a specific compatible archimedean splitting removes this witness; it does not prove the modified formula. Restoring full zero multiplicities also removes the central deficit. Multiplying all global terms by one scalar as in a Haar renormalization does not produce the corrected capped identity shown above to be false. Earlier text occasionally selects a diagonal section *for a particular estimate* (e.g. (2.2.3.8)). Neither the actual statement at p. 522 nor the discussion at pp. 528–529 imposes that restriction. The standard central point from the CM functional equation is fixed by the L-function. A character-relative central point from an arbitrary one-parameter splitting is a different notion.

The prior uses (−912,25992) and (−528,26136) and places the residue at iε. These are changes of certificate and coordinates. We claim no priority. The external revision explicitly leaves its narrow-window arithmetic comparison open and is not evidence that Masullo himself revised his text. **Verification on the actual original measures.** Over K=Q(i), the compact volume from (A.3.2.1) is V=π/4 (discriminant 4, class number one, four roots of unity, regulator one). The compact character of χ is nontrivial owing to angular type ±1, so both pole terms vanish. Comparing the unmodified p. 522 to the classical formula leaves coefficients mλ−V·1(line)min(mλ,2) multiplying B(λ)exp(λu). Finite local coefficients coincide except possibly at the one place above 2 by (A.3.2.2); its unit volume need not be assumed to be one. The support of φᵤ at any one place implies log|g|∈u+[1,2]. At the complex place, |1−g|_v≥C|g|_v for large u. The multiplicative Haar mass of that annulus is independent of u. At the finite place, |1−g|_v=|g|_v, each valuation shell has fixed Haar mass, and only a bounded number of shells occur as u varies. The tests are uniformly bounded. Thus either local difference is O(exp(−u/2)), and is bounded uniformly also on compact u intervals (the supports avoid g=1). Its Laplace transform is holomorphic for Re w>−1/2. The spectral difference has residue (m−2V)B(0)=(m−π/2)B(0)≠0 at w=0. Hence a member of the same admissible family fails the original identity too. The third-party proof gives the analogous archimedean estimate with normalized volumes; this calculation keeps the original shape of every finite unit factor, including the ramified one. This is a verification of an earlier mechanism.

## Reaudit of the existing geometric and endgame findings

**Normalization in Theorem 12.5.1.1 (pp. 519–520). Established.** The statement defines its Mellin transform with dμ on C_F. Remark 9.2.0.1 (p. 403) distinguishes this from the canonical normalization; (B.4.4.16), p. 623, divides pole and zero terms by V=μ₁(C_F¹). The zero-divisor multiplicity convention in the classical contour derivation is explicit in (B.4.2.2), p. 614, independently of whether it is indicated on every displayed sum on p. 623. For K=Q(√5), the Minkowski bound √5/2<2 gives h=1. The positive generator ε=(1+√5)/2 is a unit of norm −1. No unit 1<u<ε can have integral trace: if its norm is −1 the trace u−u⁻¹ lies strictly between 0 and 1, and if +1 then u+u⁻¹ lies strictly between 2 and 3. Thus V=2 log ε/√5. A separately implemented alternating asinh series, using log ε=asinh(1/2), reproduces the rational bounds 0.430408940964<V<0.430408940965. This is independent of the baseline script's atanh-series implementation.

Take h(t)=(t²−1)b(t)/(2√t) for a nonnegative b∈C_c^∞((1,2)), integral one in dt. Every local finite support term vanishes, including those at ramified primes: a local norm is a power q^k or 1. Both real branches must be counted. With t>1 and real Haar d×g, the sum on one real place is ½∫h(t)t^(−1/2)[1/(t−1)+1/(t+1)]dt=½. Thus K has local total 1. The canonical full zero/pole side is 1, the printed unnormalized one V. This works at width 1 since all nontrivial zeros lie in 0<Re z<1. The example requires no RH or simplicity.

**All-width scope and the three rational-quadratic tests. Established.** For K and any fixed open window, let b₀(x−u) be a translated log bump whose transform B satisfies B(1/2)>0. Subtraction of the canonical all-zero formula from the candidate printed expression gives a coefficient V−1 on the pole terms, plus ∑mλ(1−V·1in)B(λ)e^(λu). Dividing by e^(u/2), every zero term tends to zero since Re λ<1/2. Absolute summability independent of u follows by repeated integration by parts on the closed critical strip and the fixed Dedekind zero count. Thus the leading pole forces V=1 if all K-tests satisfied that width. A direct finite test uses h with compact support (1,2), once over K, twice over Q, with the second rational test twisted by the even quadratic character of conductor 5. The latter's compact projection discards pole terms and all other characters, while its real component is identically 1. Euler and gamma factorization of the completed ζ_K=ζ L(·,χ₅) gives S_K=S₁+S₅ at each window before volume factors, including common zeros and boundary exclusion. If all three identities held, 1=V(½+½). A narrow-window failure of the very same fixed bump over K alone does *not* follow from the three-probe check: the all-width K-only result allows a translated bump depending on the width. Normalizing global Haar removes the contradiction. Early public volume precedent: [issue #2469, item 17](https://github.com/mccorvie/riemann-masullo/issues/2469#issuecomment-7057757662).

**Corollary 11.1.1.4 (pp. 477–478): escaping orbit mass. Established with a notational qualification.** Use f=b(x∞)1_Zhat(x_f), where b is smooth nonnegative supported in (1/2,3/2) with b(1)=1. Put f_n(x)=f(x/n) by a *principal idèle* n. At every fixed idèle the real component tends to zero, so f_n→0. Absolute orbit sums are constant by the bijection ξ↦ξ/n of Q*. For any given g, the finite coordinates bound ξ to D_g^−1 Z for a fixed D_g; the real support then gives a finite sum J(g). Concentrate J(g) at index 1 and take fractional ideal Z in (11.1.1.8). Its right-hand side bounds each total as required. At g=1, only ξ=n contributes, with value one. The displayed type α_g:F* g→R and use of α_g(ξ) in the printed statement are reconciled via ξ↔ξg. If 1 were not the chosen representative of its idèle class, reindex by any representative in T E_F¹ of that class. The same total is one. The corollary is false; the prior report was correct to resist announcing falsity for every possible reading of Proposition 11.1.1.2. A termwise l¹-majorant on each orbit would suffice.

**Fixed rational cutoffs, pp. 480–483. Resolved false alarm.** For products of indicators applied to a single seed |f_n(ξg)|≤|f(ξg)|. For representatives g=τ(t)u with standard rational finite units, a fixed denominator D and real Schwartz bound dominate the total by C_N∑_{k≥1}(1+kt/D)^−N ≤ C'_N t^−N for N>1 (use ∑ k^−N), uniformly in unit directions. Its square is integrable against a logarithmically polynomial weight dt/t on t≥1/Λ for fixed Λ, even after a fixed norm power by choosing N larger. Thus this mechanism repairs dominated convergence for these particular vectors. It does not imply convergence of operator traces.

## Summary of changes in status

The printed identities, checked hypotheses, scalar contradictions, and limited repairs listed above survive. Two potential overstatements are expressly rejected: failure on one spectral vector cannot disprove equality of scalar traces, and the three fixed probes for each width need not isolate the same individual K-bump at every width. Mixed-adelic Schwartz non-preservation from the real–p-adic lack of a uniform additive period and the split-prime norm-stratum cancellation on p. 493 have direct public antecedents in [#2469, items 15 and 24](https://github.com/mccorvie/riemann-masullo/issues/2469#issuecomment-7057757662); neither is a new finding here. ## A Gaussian Hecke character disproves the constituent-dimension comparison on p. 533

**Target and status.** In §14.1.2, p. 533, in the unnumbered paragraph after the discussion of Galois-type characters, the paper states that the largest order \(m_F(\chi)\) of a pole of a product of Euler factors above one rational prime exceeds the dimension of each constituent of the induced Weil representation times that constituent's multiplicity. The inequality fails even with the weak comparison ≥. The definition of \(m_p(\chi)\) at the top of the same page groups places by their common *rational* residue characteristic.

**Counterexample.** Use F=Q(i). Every fractional ideal of Z[i] is principal. Define a character on fractional ideals by ψ((α))=\((\alpha/\bar\alpha)^2\); changing α by any Gaussian unit leaves the result fixed. The corresponding unitary idèle class character is explicitly χ(g)=ψ(I(g))\((\bar z/z)^2\), for complex component z and fractional ideal I(g) determined by the finite valuations. This descends to the idèle class group because the two factors cancel on diagonally embedded F*. The character is unramified at every finite place and its value on a finite uniformizer for P is ψ(P). The opposite reciprocity convention inverts every phase, with no effect on coincident pole sets.

If p is split, write \((p)=P\bar P\) with P=(π). The two finite Euler factors (1−a p⁻ˢ)⁻¹ and (1−a⁻¹p⁻ˢ)⁻¹ have a common pole exactly when a=a⁻¹, where a=\((\pi/\bar\pi)^2\). This would give \((\pi)^4=(\bar\pi)^4\), which is impossible by distinct prime-ideal valuations. Independently, if π=A+iB with A²+B²=p then the imaginary part of π⁴ is 4AB(A²−B²), nonzero for every odd split prime. Here a=π⁴/p². For instance p=5, π=2+i gives a=(−7+24i)/25. If p is inert or ramified there is exactly one place above it and its one Euler factor has simple poles. Every rational p therefore has \(m_p(\chi)=1\), so \(m_F(\chi)=1\).

By reciprocity χ supplies a character of \(W_F\), normal of index two in \(W_{\mathbb Q}\). Its conjugate is different, as seen already at p=5. The restriction of its induction to \(W_F\) is the direct sum of these two distinct characters. Any invariant line would have to be one of their eigenspaces, but an element outside \(W_F\) exchanges them. The induced representation is therefore irreducible, of dimension two and multiplicity one. The asserted comparison gives 1≥2. This calculation uses neither GRH nor a conjecture on automorphic zeros.

**Consequence, repairs and attribution.** The reason is visible on the local factors: an irreducible two-dimensional global constituent can have two distinct local eigenvalues at every split prime. The weaker comparison with the constituent's *multiplicity alone* gives 1≥1 in this example. The one-prime upper bound by the field degree still holds in this example and this objection has no identified role in the proof of the Chapter 12 narrow-window formula. It does disprove the stronger proposed relation used to interpret a possible sharpening of Chapter 13. The comparison follows discussion of Galois-type characters but the statement explicitly refers to all χ, points back to generic Proposition 14.1.2.3, and is applied to characters beyond the paragraph about Galois type. Restricting to Galois type gives the elementary upper bound from the total induced dimension, once \(m_F=[F:\mathbb Q]\) has been proved on that class. Alternative readings that omit the explicit dimension factor are unaffected.

## Further cross-checks of the analytic inputs in Chapters 3 and 7

**Proposition 3.3.1.5(3), (3.3.1.11), p. 132 — exact growth counterexample, publicly known.** For \(F=\mathbb Q(i)\), \(S=\{\infty\}\), trivial character, and \(k=0\), take \(\sigma=1\). Formula (3.3.1.19), p. 134, gives \(\rho_S(s)=(2\pi)^{1-2s}\Gamma(s)/\Gamma(1-s)\). The archimedean factors contribute the exponent \((r_1+2r_2)(\sigma-1/2)\) for their product, by Stirling's formula. Qualitative polynomial bounds on fixed vertical lines survive. The exact same example and identity were previously supplied by science-markets in [Lean project issue #2814](https://github.com/mccorvie/riemann-masullo/issues/2814).

**Lemma 7.1.2.2(1), pp. 316–317 — an extra complex gamma pole.** Use the Gaussian angular fourth-power character above and \(S=\{\infty\}\), so the sole archimedean type has \(|k|=4,\ t_\chi=0\). This is also directly a character on \(\mathbb C^\times/\mathbb Z[i]^\times\). The printed \(m\in(|k|/2)-\mathbb N\) includes \(m=-1\), even after imposing the additional \(m\le0\). Formula (3.3.1.19) gives \(\Gamma(s+2)/\Gamma(3-s)\), up to a nowhere-zero entire multiplier. At \(s=-1\) this is \(\Gamma(1)/\Gamma(4)=1/6\). Thus there is no pole at an explicitly listed parameter. The correct complex pole locations are \(-|k|/2-\mathbb Z_{\ge0}-it_\chi\). This sign repair leaves open the separate issue of summing coincident poles once.

**Theorem 7.1.2.3, (7.1.2.3), pp. 317–318 — an asymptotic missing its leading term.** Take \(F=\mathbb Q,\ S=\{\infty\},\ \chi=1,\ f(x)=\exp(-\pi x^2)\). Definition (2.3.1.2), p. 99, sums over the group \(\mathbb Z^\times=\{\pm1\}\). The compact norm-one quotient occurring in (2.3.1.13), p. 105, is trivial, of positive Haar mass \(c\). For \(g=t>0\) the exact left side is \(2c\exp(-\pi t^2)\), tending to \(2c>0\). For \(A=1/2\) the archimedean sum in (7.1.2.3) runs over positive integers \(0<m<A\), and the finite-place sum is empty. Its entire right side is \(O(t^{1/2})\). No choice of a fixed implied constant can give this estimate. One can avoid evaluating any transcendental function by choosing an even compactly supported smooth test identically one on \([-1,1]\). The left side is then exactly \(2c\) at every \(t=4^{-n}\), while the proposed upper bound is \(C2^{-n}\). Even if the index \(m\) is construed as the absolute value of a negative polar parameter, the printed \(m>0\) still removes the required \(m=0\). The Mellin transform of the Gaussian has a simple pole at zero accounting for the missing constant. In the pure real case inserting its residue repairs this test. For a general finite \(S\), include each distinct polar point just once with its full Laurent principal part. Simply adding the archimedean zero term to the existing place-indexed sums risks double counting the origin: e.g. \(S=\{\infty,2\}\) with Gaussian times \(1_{\mathbb Z_2}\) has a nonzero double pole there from both factors. Corollary 7.1.3.3 (proof p. 326) explicitly invokes \(m=0\) from the printed expansion. Its bound in this specific example is compatible with the exact value, so the false expansion alone does not disprove that bound. The manuscript calls the expansion motivation for the search for spectral bases (pp. 316, 322), and uses fixed-strip/large-prime comparisons later via separate statements.

**Theorem 7.1.3.2(2), p. 324 — an odd real pole violating the proposed multiplicity bound.** Take \(F=\mathbb Q\), \(S=\{\infty,2\}\), and the global quadratic Hecke character corresponding to the primitive character modulo four. It is ramified at two, unramified at every finite place outside \(S\), and its real component is sign. Here \(r_2=0\). At the archimedean \(m=-1\), the asserted upper bound for the polar order is \(r_2=0\). Formula (3.3.1.19) gives the real-place ratio \(\Gamma((s+1)/2)/\Gamma((2-s)/2)\), up to a nowhere-zero factor. At \(s=-1\) the numerator has a simple pole, and the denominator is \(\Gamma(3/2)\). The ramified 2-adic factor is an exponential times a nonzero root number (p. 133), producing no cancellation. The actual order is one. Lemma 7.1.2.2(1), p. 317, correctly allows real sign to have \(m=-1-2j\). Count real places of the appropriate parity as well as pole coincidences among the complex places. This finding concerns the sharper odd archimedean assertion, separately from the finite-prime degree upper bound used on p. 533.

**Theorem 7.1.4.2(1), (7.1.4.2), pp. 326–328 — product and sum yield different vector spaces.** Set \(F=\mathbb Q\) and take the trivial character, so the entire ideal generator is a nonzero multiple of Riemann's \(\xi\). The set \(D\) of its distinct zeros is countably infinite. Entire interpolation of prescribed finite jets at a closed discrete set identifies \(\mathcal O(\mathbb C)/\xi \mathcal O(\mathbb C)\) with the countable product of the finite-dimensional jet spaces. One proof uses Mittag-Leffler: assign to a meromorphic \(g\) the principal part of the desired local jet divided by \(\xi\) at each zero, and take the entire function \(\xi g\). Division by \(\xi\) identifies the kernel. The quotient and target are Fréchet, every jet evaluation is continuous, so the open mapping theorem supplies the product topology. A continuous linear functional on this product has finite coordinate support, since a product neighborhood of zero restricts only finitely many coordinates. Hence the annihilator has countable Hamel dimension. The countable product claimed in (7.1.4.2) has an uncountable independent set: choose one dimension in each jet space, enumerate the distinct zeros, and take \((1,t,t^2,\dots)\) for \(t\in\mathbb C\); any finite subset is independent by Vandermonde. There is no linear isomorphism of the stated type. Page 328 correctly computes the locally convex direct sum in the proof. A change of the statement to that sum fixes this component; a dense span of evaluations on a different space is a different assertion. The manuscript itself contains the repair. This is an independent obstruction to the printed part (1), without needing any conjecture on locations, multiplicities or spacings.
