**Scope.** This audit concerns Alessandro Maria Masullo's 940-page manuscript. The central result is a counterexample to the *value of a trace*. On a specified number field, the actual operator is trace class and the printed answer exceeds its trace by at least one. A second calculation, over the rationals, contradicts the asserted asymptotics of a Gaussian without any input about zeta zeros. These and the normalization failure proved in the preprint invalidate steps of the proposed proof of GRH. The trace-value counterexamples use odd Sobolev degree; for even degree, including the main-line choice $s=2[F:\mathbb Q]$, the printed multiplicity cap agrees with Connes's. The examples are consistent with GRH.

Six independent review assignments covered the preprint, the analytic foundations, the spectral and geometric constructions, and public antecedents. The six accompanying dossiers give the longer proofs, precise hypotheses, alternative arguments and attribution records. Page references throughout are to the original printed folios. The audit tests mathematical dependencies across Chapters 1–14 and Appendices A–E. It does not certify every numbered assertion.

# A trace whose printed value is too large

**Proposition.** Theorem 10.3.1.1(2), equations (10.3.1.3–4), p. 448, fails for $a=1/2$, Sobolev degree $s=3$, and the trivial compact character. One may specify the field and test without choosing or approximating a nonreal zero.

Put
\[
 K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5,\sqrt{21}),\qquad
 M=\frac{5+\sqrt5}{2}\frac{21+\sqrt{21}}2,\qquad F=K(\sqrt{-3M}).
\]
This is Martinet's second example, a tame quaternion extension with no integral normal basis. Fröhlich's root-number theorem gives $\zeta_F(1/2)=0$. The completed Dedekind zeta is even about $1/2$ and its other factors are regular and nonzero there. Thus $m_0=\operatorname{ord}_{1/2}\zeta_F\ge2$. No hypothesis on the other zeros is needed. Masullo already discusses multiple central zeros on p. 529. [Martinet, Example 2, p. 407](https://www.numdam.org/article/ASENS_1971_4_4_3_399_0.pdf) · [Martinet, theorem and corollary, pp. 114–115](https://www.numdam.org/item/STNG_1971-1972__1__108_0.pdf)

Let $x=\log|g|$ and let $V$ be the norm-one idèle class volume. Choose a smooth nonnegative $b$ of integral one supported in $(1,2)$; for instance normalize $\exp(-1/[(x-5/4)(7/4-x)])$ on $(5/4,7/4)$ and set it equal to zero elsewhere. Set $\widetilde b(x)=b(-x)$ and take the compact radial test $\phi(g)=V^{-1}(b*\widetilde b)(x)$. Its frequency multiplier is $B(\xi)=|\widehat b(\xi)|^2$, so $B\ge0$, $B(0)=1$, and $B$ decays rapidly. The compact average eliminates every other angular type. This theorem allows two-sided tests; a one-sided family is treated below.

Write $Z$ for the **distinct** ordinates of $\zeta_F(1/2+i\xi)=0$, and $m_\xi$ for their multiplicities. The actual and printed values, with the same Haar measure, satisfy
\[
 \operatorname{Tr} A_\phi=\sum_{\xi\in Z} B(\xi),\qquad
 \operatorname{Tr}_{\rm printed} A_\phi=\sum_{\xi\in Z}\min(m_\xi,2)B(\xi).
\]
Their difference is $\sum_{m_\xi\ge2}B(\xi)\ge1$.

**First proof.** On the central line the weighted quotient is the one in Connes's Section III. In his notation $\delta=3$; the weight in squared norm is $(1+x^2)^{\delta/2}$. His Theorem 1 counts positive integers $n<(\delta+1)/2$, up to the arithmetic multiplicity. Only $n=1$ qualifies. Corollary 2 gives trace class and the asserted sum over distinct ordinates. Convolution conventions do not affect the real even test. The manuscript's non-strict endpoint instead counts $n=2$. [Connes, Theorem 1 and Corollary 2, pp. 13–14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9811068#page=13)

**Independent proof.** In logarithmic coordinates the ambient radial space is $L^2((1+x^2)^{3/2}dx)$. Fourier transformation identifies the dual of its geometric quotient with a closed subspace $E$ of $H^{-3/2}(\mathbb R)$. A Gaussian at the infinite places times the finite integral balls unfolds to $\Lambda_F(z)$ up to a nonvanishing exponential and constant. Apply $(1-R_c)(1-cR_c)$ to the seed, where $R_c$ is idelic dilation by norm $c>1$. Its value and additive integral are now zero. The Mellin multiplier acquired is $(1-c^{-z})(1-c^{1-z})$, nonzero on the line. The quotient dual annihilates all translations of the resulting radial theta function. Fourier inversion, followed by local division away from its zeros, therefore puts the support of every $U\in E$ in $Z$.

Locally a point-supported distribution is a finite sum of delta derivatives. After cutting off a single point, its inverse transform is an exponential times a polynomial. A polynomial of degree one already has infinite squared dual norm because $\int_1^\infty x^2(1+x^2)^{-3/2}dx=\infty$. Thus only the point mass survives. Conversely each $\delta_\xi$ at a zeta zero annihilates the theta image. Indeed the unfolded zeta integral for a general seed is $\zeta_F(z)$ times a factor holomorphic locally for $\Re z>0$ after continuation. Each modified finite Euler integral differs from the unramified one by a Laurent polynomial in $q_v^{-z}$ after averaging over units, and the archimedean integrals are regular on this half-plane. The polar-free conditions justify evaluation of the full theta transform. Evaluation is continuous in the primal norm, hence it annihilates the closure.

For trace class choose a smooth partition of unity on unit frequency intervals. The localized multiplier $B_j$ has ambient operator norm $O_N((1+|j|)^{-N})$ by four integrations by parts and the polynomial translation bound. On $E$ its rank is at most the number of zero ordinates in its support, which is $O_F(\log(2+|j|))$. Project each output back into $E$ if necessary. The series converges in trace norm to the original action. For each $\lambda\ne0$, generalized eigenvectors of the multiplier are supported on the finite set $Z\cap\{B=\lambda\}$. The allowable point masses exhaust that space and are ordinary eigenvectors. Lidskii's theorem gives the first equality above. This also rules out compensation by a hidden generalized eigenvector or a negative spectral contribution. Full details and another number field appear in the spectral dossier, §3.

**Support restriction.** Even if one insists on $x>0$, take $\phi_L(g)=V^{-1}L^{-1}b(x/L)$. The excess becomes $\sum_{m_\xi\ge2}\widehat b(L\xi)$ and tends to one as $L\to\infty$. The zero count and rapid decay give a summable majorant away from $\xi=0$; conjugate symmetry gives a real excess. Thus every sufficiently large member of this admissible family fails.

## Positive-width strips

Theorem 10.3.2.1(2), pp. 453–454, claims both trace class and a strip trace formula. Use the same field, $s=3$, $a=\alpha=1/4$. The central zero is now on the upper edge. Put $v=b$, $T=A_{v/V}$ in log coordinates, $D(z)=\int v(y)e^{zy}dy$ and $R=D(\alpha)$. Since $v$ is positive on an interval at positive log norms, $|D(d+i\xi)|\le R$ on $|d|\le\alpha$, with equality only at $(\alpha,0)$. In the manuscript's predicted trace of $T^n/R^n$, every contribution but this one's tends to zero. Rapid uniform strip decay justifies summation. The predicted limit is **two**.

The weight is $e^{2\alpha|x|}(1+x^2)^{3/2}$. Translation by $y\ge0$ has norm at most $C e^{\alpha y}(1+y)^{3/2}$. Integration against $v^{*n}$ gives $r(T)\le R$. For an eigenvalue $R\omega$ with $|\omega|=1$, tilting a dual generalized eigenvector by $e^{-\alpha x}$ gives $(P-\omega)^k u=0$ with $Pu(x)=\int u(x+y)e^{\alpha y}v(y)dy/R$. The tilted vector is polynomially weighted square integrable on $x\ge0$. It also has polynomial growth on the negative half-line. To see this, iterate the averaging equation until the walk with increments in $[1,2]$ crosses zero. Its overshoot lies in $[0,2]$ and it takes at most $\lceil -x\rceil$ steps. Induction on $k$, using the same equation with inhomogeneous term $(P-\omega)u$, bounds growth by $O((1+|x|)^{k-1})$ there. The equation gives a smooth representative by convolution.

The resulting tempered distribution has Fourier support where the characteristic function of that step distribution equals $\omega$. Its modulus is strictly below one away from zero. Thus $\omega=1$ and $u$ is a polynomial; the positive half-line norm again excludes positive degree. The peripheral algebraic multiplicity is at most one. **If $T$ is trace class**, Lidskii and absolute summability of its eigenvalues give $\lim_n\operatorname{Tr}(T^n/R^n)\le1$. This disproves the conjunction. Trace class at positive width remains a separate question. The selected strip is centered at $1/4$; this calculation by itself settles neither the positive-width case centered at $1/2$ nor the degree-four trace. The corrected edge count imposes $2m+1<s$ on a jet of order $m$. The cutoff and a proposed alternative trace construction predate this audit.

**Degree five over a quadratic field.** The CM character of $\mathbb Q(i)$ constructed below has central order at least three. Averaging the test against its inverse compact character isolates that isotype; the character is trivial on the standard positive-real norm section. At $s=5$ the Hilbert dual permits polynomials of degree at most one, since the degree-two squared norm has a logarithmically divergent tail. The same one-line trace comparison counts at most two copies where the formula counts three, and the same positive-width argument at $a=\alpha=1/4$ gives a limiting peripheral multiplicity at most two versus the printed three. Thus the error also occurs with $s\ge2[F:\mathbb Q]$, the size condition used on p. 490. Section 8 of the spectral dossier spells out the character, the test and the cap. For even $s$ the printed edge count $\lfloor(s-1)/2\rfloor+1=s/2$ coincides with Connes's, so these trace-value counterexamples are confined to odd $s$. The main-line choice $s=2[F:\mathbb Q]$ is even and is not refuted by them, and the centered positive-width problem remains unresolved.

# Failures before the spectral quotient

## Finite-place continuation

Remark 2.1.2.1, p. 63, identifies the unit-invariant multiplicative Bruhat space with Schwartz sequences on the valuation lattice. Definition 5.3.4.1 and Lemma 5.3.4.2, pp. 259–261, use this space in an additive test domain and assert entireness. Over $\mathbb Q$, use $S=\{\infty,2\}$ and
\[
 h(2^n u)=
 \begin{cases}2^{-2^j}&n=4^j,\quad j\ge1,\\0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}
 \qquad u\in\mathbb Z_2^\times.
\]
Its valuation profile decreases faster than every power. It cannot extend as an additive locally constant function at zero. With unit volume one, its Mellin transform is $\sum_{j\ge1}2^{-2^j}(2^{-s})^{4^j}$. In $w=2^{-s}$ its radius of convergence is one, its exponents have consecutive ratio four, and the Hadamard gap theorem gives a natural boundary. The series is bounded on the closed disk, so no pole permits continuation through the boundary. A nonzero compactly supported archimedean Mellin factor cannot remove a natural boundary. The foundations dossier also proves the boundary by elementary derivative estimates. Restricting to compactly supported smooth multiplicative finite-place tests removes the witness. The range and topology of that restricted space then have to be justified in the later use on pp. 345–346.

## Global converse and Gaussian asymptotics

For Theorem 6.3.2.1, equations (6.3.2.10–13), pp. 304–307, take $F=\mathbb Q$, $\chi=1$ and an even smooth nonnegative $h\ne0$ supported at $1<|y|<2$. The seed $(h(y)-2h(2y))\,1_{\widehat{\mathbb Z}}$ has zero value and zero integral, exactly the two polar-free conditions. Up to common positive normalization its zeta integral is
\[
 Z(s)=(1-2^{1-s})\zeta(s)H(s)=\eta(s)H(s),\qquad H(s)=\int h(y)|y|^s d^\times y .
\]
The functional equation identifies the reflected zeta integral with $Z(s)$. The displayed gluing construction returns $((1-s)Z(s),sZ(s))$ and applies unweighted subtraction. At $s=1/4$ the result is $\eta(1/4)H(1/4)/2>0$ by the alternating series. Thus this map does not land in its asserted kernel, including on the restricted domain. The weighted condition $sF=(1-s)G$ repairs this overlap calculation. A proof of the range theorem used on pp. 326–330 and 441 still needs a valid identification of image and topology.

**An elementary counterexample to a theorem statement.** Theorem 7.1.2.3, (7.1.2.3), pp. 317–318, allows $F=\mathbb Q$, $S=\{\infty\}$, $\chi=1$, $f(y)=e^{-\pi y^2}$, $A=1/2$. The $S$-units are $\{\pm1\}$, so $\Phi(t)=2e^{-\pi t^2}\to2$ as $t\downarrow0$. The printed archimedean sum ranges over $0<m<A$; the finite-place sum is empty. The asserted bound $O(t^{1/2})$ is impossible. The theorem permits $f(0)\ne0$. This is the loss of an actual residue at zero. An explicit sequence makes the contradiction quantitative. At $t=n^{-2}$, $2e^{-\pi/n^4}\ge2(1-4/n^4)>C/n$ whenever $n\ge\max(2,C)$. The last polynomial inequality is also checked by Z3. Growth as $t$ tends to zero needs no floating-point computation.

Over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$ with both real places, the tensor Gaussian gives radial Mellin transform $c\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s/2)^2$, $c>0$, by unfolding. Even after removing the complete contribution at zero, the double pole at $s=-2$ excludes the claimed $O(t^2)$ remainder at $A=2$. Indeed the small-$t$ Mellin integral of such a remainder is $O((s+2)^{-1})$ as real $s\downarrow-2$. Equivalently the missing term has a nonzero $t^2\log t$ coefficient. A repair must include the constant and account for poles on the chosen contour. Theorem 7.1.2.3 is invoked on p. 325.

**Real-place parity.** Theorem 7.1.3.2(2), p. 324, bounds an odd negative integral polar order by the number of complex places $r_2$. For the odd character modulo four, $F=\mathbb Q$, $S=\{\infty,2\}$, the real factor at $s=-1$ is proportional to $\Gamma_{\mathbb R}(s+1)/\Gamma_{\mathbb R}(2-s)$. Its pole is simple, and the ramified factor at two is a nonvanishing exponential. Here $1>r_2=0$. Counting the odd real places repairs this example and leaves the coarser field-degree bound in play.

## Quotients, interpolation and a distinction between a span and its dual

* **Chapter 4, Theorem 4.6.1.5, p. 212.** Writing $b_k=\log|\gamma_k|$, the hypothesis controls $\log b_k$, whereas the actual spectral periods are $2\pi/b_k$. The public example takes $b_1=B,b_2=B^2$ with a super-Liouville $B$. The frequency pairs are so close that a divided-difference seminorm grows faster than the decay of a fixed Gaussian coefficient. Individual basis terms fail to tend to zero. The cross-check dossier reproduces both primal and dual arguments. The exact example was already public, down to the chosen recurrence and Gaussian. [Earlier calculation](https://github.com/mccorvie/riemann-masullo/issues/36#issuecomment-7058116453)
* **Theorem 5.4.1.1, pp. 270–274.** For two real places, the pole sets indexed by $(0,2)<_{\rm lex}(1,0)$ are respectively $\{0,-2,-4\}$ and $\{0,-2\}$, contradicting nesting. At the first index the manuscript has one Gaussian generator, whereas $\Gamma(s/2)^2$ and $s\Gamma(s/2)^2$ arise from actual tests and have independent principal parts. Norm dilates repair finite interpolation. A separate shrinking-support construction realizes every principal part in this very real-quadratic example, and handles the entire remainder. The original generation proof fails; this example supports the set-theoretic conclusion after repair. The general infinite-dimensional topology is still open here.
* **Definition 7.3.3.3, (7.3.3.5–6), p. 359.** A proposed quotient's denominator contains a vector outside its numerator. In the rational level-one case at the prime two, evaluating the theta transform of $PV_2f$ at $7/4$ gives $-1$ with the finite ball convention. With the printed unit convention, evaluation at $7/2$ gives $1/2$. Every member of the numerator vanishes at the corresponding test point. The derivation in the synthesis dossier checks support, Fourier scaling and continuity on the coinvariant. It covers both finite insertions used by the text.
* **Theorem 7.4.1.1(2), pp. 366–367.** At a repeated zero $\rho$, first-derivative evaluation annihilates the closed primal span of the selected value vectors. Multiplication by a Mellin symbol $\Phi$ with $\Phi(\rho)=0$ and $\Phi'(\rho)\ne0$ sends the value vector out of this span by the product rule. A true quotient by the kernel of the low jets is invariant in the required sense. This error concerns the selected primal span; Chapter 10 purports to supply a separate weaker route.
* **Theorem 10.4.2.8 and (10.4.2.24), p. 463.** For fixed $\alpha>0$ and $s=2$, actual simple critical-line zeta zeros force some cardinal interpolation norm up to height $T$ to be $\gg_\alpha T^{\alpha\log 2/(48\pi)}$. A length-$\alpha/4$ interval contains $k\ge\alpha\log T/(48\pi)$ such ordinates by Conrey and averaging. A disk of radius $\alpha/2$ about one node contains the other $k-1$ in its inner half. Dividing the interpolant by their finite Blaschke product and applying the maximum principle costs at least $2^{k-1}$ at the center. The Mellin transform is bounded on the disk by the weighted Hilbert norm times a fixed constant. The spectral dossier gives all constants and addresses the manuscript's parameter ambiguity. This strengthens the public close-pair bound; polynomially growing estimates may still suffice for smooth tests. [Conrey](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1989.399.1)

## What survives correcting all measures

There is also an arithmetic counterexample to the *degree cap* in Theorem 13.3.1.1 for the full set of norm sections the manuscript permits. It already appears in the independent public revision. Take $K=\mathbb Q(i)$ and $E:y^2=x^3-1254^2x$. Kummer descent sends the two points $(-1058,21896),(-912,25992)$ and the two rational torsion points with abscissas $0,1254$ to squareclasses $-2,-57,-1,1254$. These classes are $\mathbb F_2$-linearly independent in $\mathbb Q^\times/\mathbb Q^{\times2}$, as the sign and the valuations at $2,11,19$ show, so $|E(\mathbb Q)/2E(\mathbb Q)|\ge16$. Hence $\operatorname{rank} E(\mathbb Q)\ge2$. The sign is $-1$; Gross–Zagier–Kolyvagin excludes analytic rank one. Deuring's theorem for this CM curve supplies a single unitary Hecke character of $K$ with central order $m\ge3$.

Choose the norm section with complex component $\sqrt t\,e^{i\log t}$. If its angular frequency under the character is $\omega=\pm1$, the two required moments kill the test transform and its derivative at $-i\omega$, leaving the actual central zero at exponent zero visible. Explicitly use $(\partial_x-i\omega)^2h$ for $h\ge0$ smooth with positive integral supported in $(1,2)$, translate it in $x$, and multiply by the inverse character. Comparing the full canonical and capped sums and Laplace transforming the translates gives residue $(m-2)B(0)\ne0$. Other zeros give locally normally convergent terms off their own poles. With the original measures the trace dossier also computes the residual coefficient $m-\pi/2$. The ordinary positive-real section makes these moments annihilate this particular central zero. Our alternative rational-point certificate changes no priority claim. # Other established errors and their reach

* Theorem 7.1.4.2(1), pp. 326–328, identifies a continuous annihilator with a **product** of jets where its proof gives a **direct sum**. Entire interpolation allows arbitrary tuples of finite jets on the discrete zeta divisor. A continuous linear functional on their product depends on only finitely many coordinates. The two spaces have different Hamel dimensions, witnessed by the independent sequences $(1,t,t^2,\ldots)$ in the product. With the direct sum the elementary duality obstruction disappears.
* On pp. 385–386 the orbit-topology identification for the boundary confuses coordinatewise convergence in the additive adèles with the restricted-product unit topology. The synthesis dossier gives a sequence of primes escaping every finite set. We found no need to use this example in the trace contradiction.
* In Appendix B, Theorem B.3.2.1, pp. 604–606, the literal $k=0$ estimate is degenerate. Even after replacing it by a nonzero bound, good horizontal contours and two good vertical sides do not justify every other pair of pole-free sides. Pair poles at $in\pm2^{-4n^2}$ with residues $\pm2^{2n^2}$, and multiply by $2^{z^2}$. On the selected strip $0<\Re z<3/4$ only the right pole of each pair lies inside. Its weighted residue has real part $\gg2^{n^2}$, while the horizontal bounds and the original good-side bounds hold by pairwise cancellation. The absolute integral on $\Re z=0$ diverges. The missing side estimate was already known; this test disproves the unrestricted contour conclusion after the first correction. A particular Hecke product with additional bounds may admit the desired shift. [Earlier contour objection](https://github.com/mccorvie/riemann-masullo/issues/1984)
* At p. 533 the proposed bound on a constituent's dimension times its multiplicity fails for the unramified finite-place character of $\mathbb Q(i)$ defined on ideals by $\psi((\pi))=(\pi/\bar\pi)^2$. At split primes the two phases differ, since equality would imply $(\pi)^4=(\bar\pi)^4$ as ideals. Every local pole maximum is one. Its induction to $\mathbb Q$ is irreducible of dimension two because conjugation exchanges two distinct characters. This disproves the stated comparison for general characters. Separately, (14.1.1.7–9), p. 527, forms a global product over extension-field places. Applied to the trivial representation using $\mathbb Q$ or $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$ it gives $\Gamma_{\mathbb R}(s)\zeta(s)$ or $\Gamma_{\mathbb R}(s)^2\zeta_{\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)}(s)$. These differ already on the positive real axis by Stirling. Both failures concern Chapter 14's auxiliary interpretation.
* Example E.2.1.9, p. 848, asserts weak Hausdorffness for every one-point compactification of a non-locally-compact Hausdorff space. Let $D$ be uncountable and discrete and give the additional point $p$ cocountable neighborhoods. The resulting $Y$ is Hausdorff. Every infinite subset contains a countably infinite subset of $D$ closed in $Y$; the singleton cover of that closed discrete subset excludes compactness. Thus compact subsets are finite and $p$ has no compact neighborhood. In $Y^+$ infinity has cofinite neighborhoods. The subspace $D\cup\{\infty\}$ is compact Hausdorff, yet its image under inclusion omits the adherent point $p$. This refutes the universal statement. The manuscript's next example, Example E.2.1.10 (the Arens–Fort space, Hausdorff but not locally compact), already contradicts it. In Lemma E.2.1.15, p. 850, the allegedly problematic last step *is* valid: the selected discrete subset was proved closed in the compact subspace, so it is finite. Neither observation carries a deduction about zeros.

# What the endgame would still have to prove

Work backward from a zero of a fixed unitary Hecke character. With *all* canonical factors and multiplicities, subtract the usual explicit formula from an otherwise identical formula restricted to an open centered window. Character averaging and translating a one-sided bump with transform $B$ give the missing-zero distribution $\sum_{\lambda\ {\rm outside}}m_\lambda B(\lambda)e^{\lambda u}$, where $\lambda=\rho-1/2$. If it vanished for every allowed translate, its Laplace transform would vanish identically. Yet at any omitted $\lambda_0$ its residue would be $m_{\lambda_0}B(\lambda_0)$. Choosing the bump $e^{-\lambda_0 x}b(x)$ makes that value nonzero. Uniform vertical Schwartz decay and the fixed-character zero count justify both the transform and local normal convergence. Hence an identity at widths shrinking to zero would imply GRH in this component. Conversely GRH and the classical full explicit formula give those canonically normalized window identities.

The indispensable missing step is thus the *arithmetic identification of the narrowed, projected trace with the same geometric expression*. Computing a valid local trace, correcting a cap in a Hilbert dual, or interchanging two operators under an uncut trace supplies only part of this requirement. The p. 520 citation does not prove the identification. The original universal identity at the stated measure already has a scalar counterexample. The canonically normalized all-width identity has the logical force just described; the review establishes no counterexample to it.

## Open questions and resolved false alarms

**Unverified in the required generality.** Nuclearity throughout positive windows; the corrected smooth-lift estimates with growing height; global topological range after correcting the test spaces and gluing; scalar equality of alternate compressed operators after cutoffs; and transfer of arbitrary contour counterexamples to particular arithmetic integrands. The geometry at p. 520 needs an independent estimate. These are distinct claims. Failure to prove any one of them is different from a numerical contradiction to it.

**Repairs that survive our challenges.** The closed ideal rescues the one-node algebraic-division example, although the sparse pairs give another obstruction. Fixed rational orbit cutoffs admit dominated convergence. Trace cyclicity for appropriately nuclear compositions and the integrated trace-class kernel statement have valid proofs under their hypotheses. The two-real-place example admits actual principal-part interpolation. Avoiding a countable set of strip boundaries removes the discontinuous endpoint jets when choosing a sequence of widths. None of these repairs supplies the missing global comparison.
